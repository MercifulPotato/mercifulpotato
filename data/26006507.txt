It&#x27;s usually pretty easy to find stuff.\n&gt; Backtrack used to do something like this and while it was easier compared to a lot of other debian&#x2F;ubuntu based things, I still ran into some pain points.<p>FTA you linked:<p>&gt; Most Linux distributions follow the Filesystem Hierarchy Standard and declare it their own policy to maintain FHS compliance. GoboLinux and NixOS provide examples of intentionally non-compliant filesystem implementations.<p>Debian and Ubuntu adhere to FHS; they&#x27;re not more difficult in this regard than Arch or Fedora (or distributions based on these). GoboLinux and NixOS don&#x27;t because they serve a higher goal. GoboLinux for user-friendliness&#x2F;explorability and has backwards compatibility (which is hidden via optional kernel extension), NixOS aims to solve dependency hell (among various other goals). Learning curves are an obvious drawback.<p>&gt; ls -la `which &lt;program&gt;`<p>Backticks should be avoided; use ls -la $(which &lt;program&gt;) instead (avoid the $ in Fish).')
