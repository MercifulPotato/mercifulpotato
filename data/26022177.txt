Item(by='donw', descendants=None, kids=[26022441, 26022438, 26022961, 26022455], score=None, time=1612408502, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26022097, text='I came here, apparently, to agree with you strongly.<p>The ceiling in my house is, upstairs, roughly one meter above my head. Downstairs, I&#x27;ve got maybe two?<p>That is &quot;wasted space&quot;. It takes more energy to heat, and uses more building materials. Efficiency dictates that I do not need that extra airspace above my noggin, and thus it should not exist.<p>But it <i>feels</i> better.<p>When I lived in Berlin, the doors and ceilings felt as if they were built for a race of giants -- much taller, and much wider, then they needed to be.<p>And it was <i>wonderful</i>. It took some getting used to, but I could feel the difference.<p>Japan is full of inefficiency. There are people, human beings in pressed shirts and white gloves, that manually clear trains for departure on crowded platforms. Even when things aren&#x27;t crowded, there is almost always a person on every train platform in Tokyo.<p>And that feels better, too.<p>Yes, the automated systems -- which exist! -- probably do more for safety. But I really like knowing that I can scream out for help, and another human being has a job description that involves &quot;hear me&quot; and &quot;send aid.&quot; Because I&#x27;m an engineer, and I know full well how often we miss edge cases.<p>And, yes, this is less efficient. And my ticket might be a little cheaper if we had robots doing all the work. But those people do an important job. I am glad they are there, doing that job. I really don&#x27;t want to those jobs &quot;efficiented&quot; away by a bunch of management experts who are chauffeured about in cars with tinted windows, and never ride the trains anyway.<p>Games are inefficient. But they make us happy.<p>Love is inefficient. One could make he argument that it would be better for society if computers assigned relationships based genetic&#x2F;personality&#x2F;economic&#x2F;whatever criteria. But I don&#x27;t think any of us want that, either. Gattica was a <i>dystopia</i>.<p>I&#x27;m not sure how, but we need to capture the idea that human happiness requires that every system have, built-in, a certain amount of inefficiency.')
