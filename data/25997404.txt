Item(by='themacguffinman', descendants=None, kids=[25998159], score=None, time=1612239704, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25996628, text='Most people won&#x27;t have to &quot;get over&quot; latency because they&#x27;re not used to or sensitive to low latency in the first place. I&#x27;m not saying their eyes aren&#x27;t good enough or something, but the fact is that a majority of them either can&#x27;t or won&#x27;t spend on gear necessary for the performance you&#x27;re claiming. Steam&#x27;s own hardware survey indicates that the most basic, cheap monitors are the norm with 1080p or lower being the most common resolution; refresh rate isn&#x27;t explicitly shown but I&#x27;d be surprised if 120+hz is even above single digits of marketshare. Many gamers don&#x27;t even play on PCs, they play on consoles running games as low as 30fps with cheap TVs that often don&#x27;t have &quot;Game Mode&quot; turned on which alone can eliminate more than 8ms of latency.<p>Tactical shooters and MOBAs are still immensely popular <i>despite</i> the fact that most consumers likely experience a fairly high level of latency. People play games like Fortnite because they&#x27;re fun, not because they&#x27;re so competitive &amp; fast paced that 8ms of latency makes a difference. Even competitive games nowadays try to soften the direct correlation between player skill and wins using matchmaking and RNG mechanics so everyone gets a reasonably consistent rate of winning. Want to play a really competitive and fast paced game where 8ms probably does matter? Play Quake Live against the 100 or so people that still tolerate that level of competition.')
