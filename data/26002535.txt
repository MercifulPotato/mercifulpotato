Item(by='pwdisswordfish6', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612281616, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26001179, text='Just an observation, here&#x27;s something that&#x27;s a result of the fact that self-published opinion pieces on the Internet don&#x27;t necessarily go through an editorial process before publication:<p>&gt; It has a real cost, you know, being a dick to maintainers. [...] We’re out here trying to make things better [...] We’ve sacrificed our spare time to build this for you for free.<p>Yes, but who are you addressing?  There&#x27;s a subtext here that discouraging behavior can be such a burden that it takes away resources from the project.  Perhaps it takes such a toll that a maintainer just calls it quits on the whole thing.  That makes sense when addressing your fans who are consuming your work but nonetheless burdening you.  To a detractor, who isn&#x27;t using Wayland and doesn&#x27;t want Wayland to succeed, discouragement leading to burnout is not failure condition.  This logical slip is probably the result of leaning <i>too</i> heavily on the vaccine metaphor.<p>Now playing Devil&#x27;s advocate (contra this piece):<p>In the real world, anti-vaxxers benefit no matter what they believe.  But that&#x27;s not so in Wayland v. X11 duel, which is not only a genuine a matter of preference, but there are actually shades of a zero-sum relationship here.  The idea of Wayland, like a vaccine, being good for you whether you know it or not doesn&#x27;t hold up.  If you&#x27;re in the X11 camp and oppose Wayland, improvement to the Wayland ecosystem isn&#x27;t bringing nice things like herd immunity.  It&#x27;s actually a threat if your vision is one where X11 remains entrenched for the foreseeable future.  It would also be really easy for a detractor of Wayland to use this post for ammunition, for example labelling this as self-aggrandizement and maybe claiming that there&#x27;s an attitude where Waylanders see themselves as saviors, rescuing others from a plight they don&#x27;t even agree exists, and that it&#x27;s an attitude indicative of the project as a whole.')
