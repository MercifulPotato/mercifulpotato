Item(by='Jtsummers', descendants=None, kids=[26021362], score=None, time=1612384921, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26018643, text='You&#x27;ll gain <i>hours</i>, but not necessarily <i>productivity</i>. Which is a common mistake in management thinking. An extra hour of work out of an assembly line can produce an extra hour&#x27;s worth of product, there&#x27;s a pretty clear and measurable extra output there. In software, and a lot of other design work, this correspondence is less precise. For Cody the Coding Machine you may get a real extra hour of output from him. From many others, you&#x27;ll find they extend their breaks from 15 minutes to 20 minutes, lunch goes from 30 to 45 minutes, and the extra time is taken up by meetings or answering emails.<p>The productivity doesn&#x27;t come from extra time, it comes from alleviating stress and increasing the ability to focus. If my thoughts at 2pm are, &quot;Wait, is it my day to pick up the kids? I&#x27;ve got a 3pm meeting, I&#x27;ve got to text the wife to let her know I can&#x27;t get them today.&quot; I&#x27;m unfocused. Childcare, consequently, can relieve me of those thoughts. Same thing with bringing in food or having medical and fitness options available. They relieve some stress&#x2F;unproductive time spent thinking and planning, which will hopefully improve productivity overall, but extra hours recovered from this are not necessarily more productive just by being available.')
