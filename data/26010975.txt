Item(by='cco', descendants=None, kids=[26011352], score=None, time=1612331582, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26010851, text='This is nearly always missed in my experience. A good product is &quot;winning&quot; for your engineering team, if you do not have a good product, you have lost. Would you have made a good product if your team spent less time in the locker room(meetings)? Maybe, and certainly more time on the field probably will increase your odds of winning, but it won&#x27;t ensure it.<p>This always takes me to USE YOUR PRODUCT. If you don&#x27;t use your product, you&#x27;ll never know if it is good. My team builds internal tools for our support folks, we also use these tools every day to try and answer our own questions about internal problems. Are the tools we make getting better? Well, just consult our &quot;How long does it take to answer X?&quot; KPI, we have a set of known common problems; if our support folks are spending less time figuring out the answer to these gold standards, our product is improving, if they&#x27;re spending longer, we&#x27;ve regressed and we need to change something.<p>I&#x27;ll grant that making internal tools grants us a lot of gimmes, we&#x27;re not tasked with taking advantage of users, and we can spend time training users intensively on new releases, but the underlying principle is the same; a chef will never know if a recipe is good without tasting it himself, and you&#x27;ll never know if your team is successful if you don&#x27;t know if the product you&#x27;ve created is good.')
