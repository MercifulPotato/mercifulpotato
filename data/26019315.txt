Item(by='bsanr2', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612387536, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26015535, text='I think you&#x27;ll find that most people who focus on identity politics would rather have that gap closed for everyone, than to see specifically people who look like them in places of power why they themselves continue to languish. The issue is that these people who focus on identity politics aren&#x27;t actually stupid and, in fact, do understand the rules of this game that they were swept into (without their consent, mind you): power accrues more power and tribalism is rampant. Under the status quo, people who&#x27;ve been marginalized in the past have a better chance when they increase their numbers in seats of power. Further, strides forward in economic and social wellbeing tend to leave them behind unless they speak up.<p>So the dilemma in the situation you describe is between whether to ignore diversity concerns and in turn <i>be</i> ignored, or to speak up and risk losing class solidarity. It sucks that we have to make that decision; it would be better if less-marginalized people made proactive efforts to ensure we&#x27;re involved in and will explicitly benefit from initiatives or movements that implicitly aligns with our interests.')
