Item(by='zzbzq', descendants=None, kids=[26003678], score=None, time=1612279676, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26001511, text='I don&#x27;t think it&#x27;s hotly debated. It&#x27;s frivolously debated. Occasionally someone acknowledges that certain formulae or constants might actually be variable, but ultimately move on to assume they are fixed. But even in those hypothetical scenarios it&#x27;s assumed that the laws of nature would evolve by some meta-laws. I&#x27;ve never seen it considered that there are no laws at all, despite the fact that this seems to me the most likely case. The existence of laws is supposed as a prerequisite for the method of science. I think calling it faith is apt because the idea that the universe works by &quot;laws&quot; is, at its inception, a religious idea! Newton after all saw science as a religious endeavor.')
