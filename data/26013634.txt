Item(by='davidkpiano', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612360879, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26011811, text='Here&#x27;s the funny thing: they <i>are</i> absolutely everywhere! It&#x27;s just that the vast majority of them are implicit.<p>What I mean by this is you can describe virtually every piece of &quot;logic&quot; in an app as a state machine - there are &quot;behaviors&quot; (finite states) that might change (transitions) due to something that happens (events).<p>The problem is that there are many ways developers wire those up, where the finite states, transitions, and events are there, but it&#x27;s difficult to enumerate them clearly.<p>So why doesn&#x27;t everyone use <i>explicit</i> state machines? Simply because languages make it easier to write implicit state machines (e.g., async&#x2F;await), and it&#x27;s the path of least resistance to getting code to &quot;just work&quot;. Unfortunately, most developers care about the frictionless path rather than the most robust path, especially in this startup-driven world.')
