I don&#x27;t speak for Google.<p>That lawsuit alleges that Google was tracking people (with analytics) during incognito mode, which is different from opting out of ad personalization. See <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=23552967" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=23552967</a> for HN discussion on the topic.<p>If you use Firefox, you can install <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;addons.mozilla.org&#x2F;en-US&#x2F;firefox&#x2F;addon&#x2F;interest-advertising-opt-out&#x2F;" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;addons.mozilla.org&#x2F;en-US&#x2F;firefox&#x2F;addon&#x2F;interest-adve...</a> to also opt out of ad-personalization.<p>Turning off ad personalization doesn&#x27;t turn off tracking; it just prevents that data from being used in ads. If you want to turn off data collection altogether (for your Google account) you can manage activity altogether <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;myactivity.google.com&#x2F;activitycontrols" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;myactivity.google.com&#x2F;activitycontrols</a>. (I don&#x27;t know whether this affects analytics; I assume it does, though.)<p>If you want to disable analytics tracking when you&#x27;re logged out, you can install another extension (<a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;tools.google.com&#x2F;dlpage&#x2F;gaoptout" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;tools.google.com&#x2F;dlpage&#x2F;gaoptout</a>) or just install a content blocker like uBlock Origin that blocks the analytics.js&#x2F;gtag.js scripts altogether.')
