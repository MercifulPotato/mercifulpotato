Item(by='hunter2_', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612305982, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26006143, text='Yes, field of view is what really matters, but scaling at the OS level (like TFA&#x27;s author is doing) is a good way to correct for unusual fields of view. Got a higher-than-typical DPI and&#x2F;or ridiculously far away screen and&#x2F;or vision impairments? Scale the OS up. Got a screen with lower-than-typical DPI and&#x2F;or touching your nose and&#x2F;or amazing vision? Scale the OS down. You need the entire OS scaled, not just web pages.<p>&quot;CSS pixels&quot; are kind of a roundabout way to conceptualize it, but they solve the problem of proper readability once the OS scale is correct for the user. The combination acts as a proxy for field of view.<p>Then there&#x27;s detection of course&#x2F;fine pointing devices, as already discussed, to further augment the layout for proper target area.<p>I don&#x27;t believe anything else will matter if the above is accounted for.')
