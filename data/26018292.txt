Item(by='ahepp', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612381877, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26011758, text='How the union acquires their stake is a fair question which could be answered a lot of different ways. It can simply be considered a part of employee compensation. This is normal for software engineers (RSUs). The union might get a loan to purchase a stake. I worked for a company that transitioned from private to employee owned, it&#x27;s not an insurmountable problem by any means.<p>Unless I&#x27;m seriously misunderstanding you, what you&#x27;re calling a &quot;conflict of interest&quot; is literally my entire point.<p>It is a good thing that the union has an incentive to balance the interests of workers with the interests of the overall company.<p>If the company needs to cut jobs to be competitive, it is a good thing that the union has a stake in both sides of the issue. That way they will be incentivized to work towards a solution that is fair to the workers, and profitable for investors.<p>It&#x27;s the exact situation we all face every day. I like the taste of donuts. I know that if I eat them all the time, I&#x27;ll get fat. Because my stomach and brain have a &quot;conflict of interest&quot;, I make choices that balance short term and long term pleasure. If every donut I made got somebody else fat, then they would be in trouble, because I might like the taste of donuts more than I care about their health.')
