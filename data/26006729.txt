Item(by='PeterisP', descendants=None, kids=[26009581], score=None, time=1612300281, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26006427, text='So customer A is doing stuff where you need to put up a collateral with some counterparty.<p>You can&#x27;t use customer B&#x27;s money (this is a key assumption that might be missing - it&#x27;s all about the use of <i>other</i> customers money) for that collateral because, well, that collateral might not get returned in certain cases - that&#x27;s kind of the point of having a collateral. You&#x27;d lose that collateral if the counterparty goes belly up (insolvency, fraud, whatever), and, most importantly, you&#x27;d lose that collateral if you become insolvent. That&#x27;s not OK - this is regulated so that you are required to ensure separation of &quot;your money&quot; from &quot;customers money that you&#x27;re holding on their behalf&quot;, so that the customer&#x27;s money is untouched and unclaimed even you go bankrupt. It&#x27;s not your money, it&#x27;s the customer&#x27;s money that you&#x27;re investing on their behalf, so you can&#x27;t put it up as repayment or collateral for your liabilities; and you can&#x27;t put one customer&#x27;s money as repayment or collateral for another customer&#x27;s liabilities.')
