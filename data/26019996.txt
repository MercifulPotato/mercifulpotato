Item(by='marsdentech', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612391125, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26014831, text='I used to run a department at a biotech where ~50% of our data came from MinIONs (although, that said, I&#x27;m a bioinformatician, rather than a molecular biologist), so I can answer your questions. For (a.), you can for sure &quot;batch&quot; samples. The term of art you&#x27;re looking for is &quot;multiplexing&quot;. Nanopore provide prep kits that allow you to &quot;barcode&quot; different samples (i.e. tag all the molecules in a given sample with a unique, synthetic sequence, which allows them to be distinguished by software downstream), but note that (as with all DNA prep kits, but some more than others) you&#x27;ll need access to a fair whack of lab equipment and consumables to use it (these kits aren&#x27;t &quot;all-in&quot;). For (b.), for one anecdata point, I used to process a whole flow cell&#x27;s data on an M4800 with a 4th Gen i7 and 32 GB of RAM in a few hours. Most of the &quot;high&quot; computational requirements you hear about relate to either assembly or variant calling (both of which are downstream of just retrieving &quot;usable&quot; sequencing data); and even both of those I&#x27;ve managed on that same laptop overnight. Actually acquiring the data (you can delay base calling if you like, although you probably wouldn&#x27;t need to) is real-time and only needs very modest hardware (IMHO the Nanopore &quot;system requirements&quot; are very much on the &quot;safe-side&quot;.) &quot;In the field&quot;, your challenge would be physically preparing the samples!')
