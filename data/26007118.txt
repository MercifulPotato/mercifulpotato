Item(by='Veedrac', descendants=None, kids=[26008901], score=None, time=1612301812, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26005871, text='The issue with x86 is that instructions can be any length, and that their length is a nontrivial function of their encoding. This means there&#x27;s an unavoidable sequential scan over, effectively, every byte of the input when doing decode. (This isn&#x27;t implemented as a sequential scan like it would be in software.)<p>With RISC-V, compressed instructions are still 16 bit, and all instructions are 16 bit aligned. Further, the instruction length is fully determined by the first two bits in the instruction (11₂ for 32 bits).<p>For an 8-wide decoder, this means routing is fully determined by 15 bits, after a single AND reduction, this can be expanded early during a fetch pass, the worst case is an 8-way MUX for the last instruction, and even doing decode fully in parallel at every possible offset would at worst require interleaving eight 32 bit decoders with eight (much simpler) 16 bit decoders. None of that really matters a great deal since RISC-V is so trivial to decode.')
