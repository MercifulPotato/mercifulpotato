Item(by='StillBored', descendants=None, kids=[26019006], score=None, time=1612380280, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26017771, text='Make isn&#x27;t great, but if you look at the opensource world the vast majority of the large projects are make based. The backbone of your average distro is a thin layer on top of what is mostly automake&#x2F;autoconf in the individual projects. That is because while I can create a toy project that builds with make in a half dozen lines, big projects can extend it to cover those odd edge cases that break a lot of other &quot;better&quot; build systems. Particularity, when a project starts including a half dozen different languages.<p>So, while i&#x27;m not a make fan, I&#x27;m really tired of people pissing on solutions (c also comes to mind) that have been working for decades because of edge cases or problems of their own creation because they don&#x27;t understand the tool.<p>A well understood tool is one where people know where the problems lie and work around them. Most &quot;perfect&quot; tools are just project crashing dragons hiding under pretty marketing of immature tooling.')
