Item(by='pdonis', descendants=None, kids=[26009064], score=None, time=1612311691, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26008353, text='<i>&gt; Claiming a different definition of free will does not make you right about free will existing.</i><p>You are missing my point. I explicitly said that I used a <i>different</i> term, &quot;making choices&quot;, <i>instead of</i> the term &quot;free will&quot;, exactly <i>because</i> it allows one to avoid all the pointless arguing about whether free will &quot;exists&quot; or not.<p><i>&gt; Defining free will as &quot;making choices&quot;, such as building a fire to warm your house because you are cold is not proving free will exists.</i><p>Again, you are missing the point. Sure, you can say that &quot;free will&quot; doesn&#x27;t exist because &quot;particles react to their environment&quot;. And my response is, who cares? Sure, people are ultimately made of particles reacting to their environment. That doesn&#x27;t mean they can&#x27;t make choices, and it doesn&#x27;t mean the choices they make don&#x27;t make a difference, to themselves and to others. It doesn&#x27;t mean that some people&#x27;s bad choices don&#x27;t cause other people to suffer. It is perfectly possible to <i>both</i> understand that, at a microphysical level, everything is &quot;particles reacting to their environment&quot;, <i>and</i> that, at a personal level, people make choices and their choices have consequences. And if you focus solely on the former and ignore the latter, the result is <i>worse</i> consequences, not better ones.')
