That&#x27;s pretty unique.<p>Compare that to Android where you can develop on a large number of platforms,  or the many cross-platform tools that can target Windows, MacOS and Linux. Back in the day Infocom developed games like Zork on a PDP-11 and published them to home computers.  It&#x27;s a normal thing in the industry unless you&#x27;re developing for a monopolist.<p>Similarly iMessage is sneaky and substitutes for SMS -- my sister-and-law noticed this the other day and was suprised.  Thus the system shuns and isolates non-iPhone apple users from their friends and shames and intimidates them to switch.<p>Android is about as much a real competitor for iOS as MacOS was in the late 1990&#x27;s when Microsoft bought a stake in Apple to keep Apple alive so the antitrust courts wouldn&#x27;t tear them apart.<p>Google doesn&#x27;t make money on Android,  Android phone makers don&#x27;t make a profit despite selling $2400 phones.  The real economic value of Android is that it&#x27;s an atomic bomb pointed at Apple.  If Google decided to end Android the way that they end most platforms,  the EU would kick Apple out in a heartbeat.  So Apple owes a lot to Google (patronage network style) for keeping an unviable OS alive.')
