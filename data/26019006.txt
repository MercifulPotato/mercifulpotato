Item(by='klodolph', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612385840, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26017957, text='&gt; …I&#x27;m really tired of people pissing on solutions (c also comes to mind) that have been working for decades because of edge cases or problems of their own creation because they don&#x27;t understand the tool.<p>You would have to be extraordinarily cynical to think that there’s been no progress in making better build software in the past forty years or so.<p>Yes, there are definitely plenty of build systems out there that were built from scratch to “fix” make without a sufficient understanding of the underlying problem. I’m not going to name any; use your imagination. But make was never <i>that good</i> at handling large projects to begin with, and it was never especially good at ensuring builds are correct. This is not “pissing on make”, for Chris’s sake, make is from 1976 and it’s damn amazing that it’s still useful. This is just recognizing make’s limitations, and one of those limitations is the ability for make to handle large projects well <i>in general,</i> specific cases notwithstanding. Part of that is performance issues, and these are tied to correctness. As projects which use make grow larger, it becomes more likely that they run into problems which either interfere with the ability to run make recursively or the ability to run make incrementally.<p>The “poor craftsman who blames his tools” aphorism is a nice way to summarize your thoughts about these kind of problems sometimes, and it does apply to using tools like make and C, but it’s also a poor craftsman who chooses tools poorly suited to the task.')
