Item(by='marsdentech', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612390170, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26014998, text='This is a common, and often justified, though not always fair, criticism. MinIONs have an error rate of around 10% for _any given base_. Moreover, these errors aren&#x27;t entirely independent of one another, so if you struggle to sequence a given base the first time, you&#x27;re likely also to struggle if you try again. That said, if your experiment is such that you&#x27;re only sequencing a guaranteed single target (e.g. one, isolated coronavirus genome), in that one sequencing run (on that one flow cell), you&#x27;ll &quot;re-sequence&quot; the same any given region many times and, unless you&#x27;re looking at &quot;problematic&quot; (i.e. low-complexity) regions, you _will_ be able to &quot;average out&quot; the errors to reveal the true target sequence. On the other hand, if you&#x27;re trying to co-sequence a mixture of closely-related targets, that&#x27;s when the headache starts...')
