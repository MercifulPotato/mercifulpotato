Item(by='boublepop', descendants=None, kids=[26004747, 26012337], score=None, time=1612288751, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25998807, text='&gt; Similarly, the equations of quantum mechanics predict multiple &quot;worlds&quot;<p>No they don’t. If I roll a dice it’ll land on one of 6 faces. That’s a model, in that model I might assume equal probability due to the geometrical symmetry, that’s an assumption. So now I have a problem if all outcomes are equally likely how do I resolve that only one occurs? Well obviously every dice roll must spawn 6 parallel universes... Now nowhere in that model or that assumption does “so there must be 6 parallel worlds spawned when you roll the dice” get predicted by my model. The multiverse&#x2F;many world is an interpretation, not an outcome of, or even prediction from the model.<p>There is nothing “right there in the math”. QM makes predictions about probabilistic outcomes, accurate predictions, but there is no requirement for those accurate predictions to be right that there must be multiple parallel worlds.')
