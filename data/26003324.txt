Item(by='rwmj', descendants=None, kids=[26004353, 26005750, 26005619, 26004050], score=None, time=1612284763, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26001972, text='A lot of nonsense in this article, but I&#x27;ll try to address his points:<p>(1) There are several royalty free implementations, ranging from the minimal (PicoRV32) to widely-used embedded (Rocket) to reasonably high-end (BOOMv*).  There are also proprietary implementations, and this is how it should be.<p>(2) RISC-V has defined various platform specifications which means in practice ISA fragmentation is not going to be a thing.  The platform will define the minimum set of extensions, and the rest will be probed at runtime (which is exactly the way x86 works).<p>(3) About economics - the author ought to read some Clayton Christiansen, or maybe take a look at how companies like Intel, AMD and Arm came to prominence.<p>(4) Yes, open spec != open implementation, but see point (1).<p>(5) RISC-V has been designed by experts in the field and adopted by some very large hardware companies with experience and who ship in very large volumes (millions and billions of chips).  It may well not be perfect, but it doesn&#x27;t need to be in order to be successful (see also x86).')
