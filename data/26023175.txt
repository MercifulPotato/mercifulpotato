This header allows clients behind NAT to be identified with significantly greater precision.<p>That doesn&#x27;t make the X-Client-Data header identifying. Or, if it does, then your definition of &quot;identifying information&quot; is exactly equivalent to your definition of &quot;information&quot;, because it is true that any particular axis or piece of data can be used to identify clients with greater precision. The rest is just <i>how</i> identifying the information is. Country is identifying, as is first digit of IP address, but both are less identifying than MAC address or first name. You&#x27;re welcome to subscribe to such a definition, but it&#x27;s not the normal one (which is usually that said piece of data can be tied to a specific individual, although you may not be able to correlate that particular individual with a name or similar).<p>Under this definition, I&#x27;d probably agree that the combination of IP address and X-client-data header is identifying, but that&#x27;s also not a thing anyone is going to be using, because it&#x27;s not particularly useful.<p>&gt; There is. Their business lives and dies on their ability to track web users<p>Sort of. The value of tracking individual users at the granularity you suggest is, as far as I know, dubiously valuable, and importantly, there&#x27;s no reason to believe it&#x27;s done. Like there&#x27;s no evidence to support this beyond the conjecture.<p>&gt; This is (obviously) about the hundreds of millions of Chrome users who are not logged in, and as I said above, 13 bits of entropy isn&#x27;t marginal.<p>But if you&#x27;re in this situation, and again, Google is being unethical, there are a host of other tracking tools that provide 13 or more bits of entropy that they can use.<p>&gt; Nobody &quot;lied&quot; per se, but the end result is the same.<p>I&#x27;d classify that as lying (for actually more than one reason). But I&#x27;ll just leave you with the same point I made to 0xy the last time this was brought up: this X-client-Data header is nearly 9 years old. When it was introduced it was considered nonidentifying and was used for chrome experiments. It is still considered nonidentifying and used for chrome experiments.<p>And this is why the whole idea doesn&#x27;t make sense: if the header was introduced now, I could see this train of thought making sense. But it wasn&#x27;t recently introduced. They&#x27;ve had more than 8 years to use it while there have been all kinds of other ways to get additional bits.<p>So now your chain of reasoning is more like<p>1.
