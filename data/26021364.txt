Item(by='gerdesj', descendants=None, kids=[26021638, 26022040, 26022539], score=None, time=1612400926, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26018805, text='&quot;What could a mathematician do with a perfect shuffle?&quot;<p>Perfect?  That&#x27;s a word which should not be deployed lightly!<p>By definition a shuffle can never be &quot;perfect&quot; - a card shuffle should strive to mix up the cards but it can never do that job and then say &quot;these cards are optimally shuffled&quot;<p>Oh so now we have a new definition for perfect: &quot;Praeger, however, is interested in just those permutations of the cards that you can get by using perfect shuffles, when a pack of cards is exactly split in half and the cards from each half perfectly interleaved one by one.&quot;<p>I have a different idea of what perfect means and it is not that.  I suggest the word &quot;precise&quot; or &quot;exact&quot; when describing fancy shuffling schemes.')
