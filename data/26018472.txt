Item(by='jfengel', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612382915, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26014768, text='I think it&#x27;s mostly just a way of making the show more visually interesting. Take a look at those dance scenes, and then compare it to a shot from any other regency romance movie. The faces in the latter all just blend in to one another; everything looks like a big blur.<p>The ethnicity is largely ignored. There&#x27;s one interesting passing reference to it -- the idea that it&#x27;s a recent phenomenon that could easily be reversed -- but they spend little time on it.<p>It&#x27;s not authentic, but of course nothing about Bridgerton is authentic. Queen Charlotte&#x27;s skin color is no wronger than her clothing, which is more 1770s France than 1810s England (and not really authentic at that, either). The Duke of Hastings&#x27; skin exactly matched that of the real Duke of Hastings -- since Hastings was a Barony.')
