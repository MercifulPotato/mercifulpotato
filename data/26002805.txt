Item(by='StillBored', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612282689, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25988985, text='Well there are mITX boards with 8 or more SATA ports, and a lot of mATX boards with the same.<p>But the key differentiator IMHO is ECC and 10G. If one picks a board like <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.gigabyte.com&#x2F;Motherboard&#x2F;C246N-WU2-rev-10" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.gigabyte.com&#x2F;Motherboard&#x2F;C246N-WU2-rev-10</a> it then becomes possible to put a i3-9100&#x2F;etc on it and gain ECC support. Given the 8 onboard SATA ports, and a 10G+ nic in the PCIe slot, you have a NAS that can best nearly everything on the market below a couple thousand dollars. There of course is a pretty wide range of atom&#x2F;xeon boards with BMC&#x27;s, and all of the above including the 10G. In the past I ran a  <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.asrockrack.com&#x2F;general&#x2F;productdetail.asp?Model=C2550D4I" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.asrockrack.com&#x2F;general&#x2F;productdetail.asp?Model=C...</a> which has 12 sata ports, ECC and a BMC.<p>But, yes it will consume more than a rpi, and CPU power rating isnt everything. Having 8+ sata ports, bmcs, 10G ethernet, etc all up the board power consumption. But on the other hand, that i3 is rated for 65W, but idles at ~5 on a modern board, which is in the ballpark of the 2-3W of my RPi4 (which only swings a watt or so, and doesn&#x27;t include the USB3 hub+devices).<p>So, given the NAS spends a lot of its time idle, and the power only spikes (and never to anything close to 65W) when its being used, it seems the extra power is worth the extra performance. I could probably fix the max frequency at 2Ghz like the RPi and save even more power.')
