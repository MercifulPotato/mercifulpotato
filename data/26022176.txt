This pattern would have a start and end block indicating it is &quot;new&quot;.<p>Function 2:\nRelatively rare protein finds RNA marked &quot;new&quot; and replaces start and end blocks with &quot;old&quot; markers.<p>Function 3:\nCommon protein grabs one strand marked new, and one strand marked old and compares part of the tail of &quot;new&quot; to the head of &quot;old&quot; by sliding them past each other.<p>If a match is found, the older strand is marked &quot;match&quot; at the start.<p>Function 4:\nMatch strands are used to trigger the next several firings of the neuron, then marked &quot;hold&quot;<p>Function 5:\n&quot;Old&quot; strands are gradually recycled.<p>Function 6:\n&quot;Hold&quot; strands are copied into new &quot;old&quot; strands for comparison to &quot;new&quot; strands by function 3<p>Function 7:\nSomething to do with continual refinement of &quot;hold&quot; strands with incoming &quot;new&quot; strands.<p>...<p>Anyway, this whole little system just popped into my head based on the small amount I know about crispr and cellular machinery (which honestly isn&#x27;t much), and I wanted to share.')
