Item(by='labawi', descendants=None, kids=[26006555, 26006354], score=None, time=1612295769, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26003052, text='Each allocation also needs an entry in the page table (actually a virtual memory tree). On x86&#x2F;amd64 the fanout is about 1:512.<p>If you have 8MiB stacks, then a minimally allocated stack uses a 4KiB data page, but also 2MiB of address range uses up a full 4KiB bottom level page table, and 8MiB range takes up 4&#x2F;512 x 4KB of 2nd level page table and so on. So you use about 8.03 KiB RAM if you never touch more than 4KB and your 8MB reservations are mostly grouped. Some architectures have bigger pages, increasing fanout but also the minimum allocation.<p>Contrast to 2KiB stacks without reservations&#x2F;overcommit - you use about 2KiB of usable RAM + (1&#x2F;2) x (1&#x2F;512) of 4KiB 1st level page table + .. , assuming allocation are again mostly grouped. Hence, for up to 2KiB of stack memory you need about¹ 2.005 KiB of RAM. Works the same for 16 KiB and even 64 KiB page sizes.<p>100000 * (8MiB reserved, 1..4KiB used stack) needs ~784MiB RAM.<p>100000 * (2KiB reserved, 1..2KiB used stack) needs ~200MiB RAM.<p>Note that, if you actually touch your reserved stack, even once, your allocation can balloon to possibly tens or hundreds of GiB (100k * 8MiB = ~800GiB), unless you do complicated cleanup, while a segmented stack can keep the allocations within reasonable efficiency, freeing any excessive stack allocations in userspace.<p>¹ ignoring bookkeeping overhead in both cases, to keep the calculation clear. Hopefully it isn&#x27;t more than a dozen bytes or so.')
