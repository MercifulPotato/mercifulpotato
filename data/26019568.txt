Item(by='xyzzy123', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612388702, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26014525, text='Not a huge fan of offline-attackable password hashes in datastores.<p>I want to put a vote in here for an anchoring. Lots of methods:<p>1) Code secret. This is ok but loses all strength if disclosed.<p>2) KMS wrapping of hashes. This is better; attacker needs to be in your environment, actively unwrapping all your stuff.<p>3) Controlled hashing step (best).  Have one step of your password hashing incorporate a one-way operation using a &quot;normally non-extractable&quot; hardware secret. This could be in CloudHSM if you&#x27;re in AWS.<p>This completely prevents offline attacks unless the attacker compromises your HSM.<p>4) It would be nice if cloud providers implemented &quot;password hashing as a service&quot;. They could easily and cheaply prevent offline attacks.<p>5) Of course strong key auth instead of passwords for clients would be nicer.')
