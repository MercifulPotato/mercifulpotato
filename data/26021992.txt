They give the answer in the article itself, although they don&#x27;t appear to notice what they just wrote:<p>&quot;Serological surveys — random testing for antibodies — show that a majority of people in certain areas of India may have already been exposed to the coronavirus, without developing symptoms...But many experts caution that herd immunity — a controversial term, they say — would only begin to be achieved if at least 60% to 80% of the population had antibodies.&quot;<p>So, you have multiple seroprevalence studies showing over 50% already exposed in crowded areas, and you have reason to think you need at least 60% to get herd immunity.  Given that there have been multiple simulations that suggest it could be substantially less, it seems like covid-19 cases in India are probably going down for the classic reason that every pandemic ends, which is that it goes through a standard SIR curve (<a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;en.wikipedia.org&#x2F;wiki&#x2F;Compartmental_models_in_epidemiology#The_SIR_model" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;en.wikipedia.org&#x2F;wiki&#x2F;Compartmental_models_in_epidem...</a>).  I mean India&#x27;s confirmed cases curve looks almost exactly like an SIR curve from a textbook.  There&#x27;s not a whole lot that needs explaining when something happens just about the exact way that, in 2019 and before, we would have said it would happen.  We&#x27;re just so freaked out in the middle of the pandemic that it is difficult for a reporter to just say that, apparently.')
