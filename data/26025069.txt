Item(by='jet_32951', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612444793, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26023827, text='Service might simply be seen as better because of cultural differences between your home country and one you visit. Example: in France Americans often perceive restaurant service as slow and indifferent, because the French service is not about feeding you and getting you the hell out of the restaurant. The word &quot;restauration&quot; in French implies a restoring (of not just your stomach, but your well-being) and that is what French restaurants are <i>for</i>. Allowing you to be seated and to look around you for a while, discussing rather than quickly selecting what is on offer for the meal, a leisurely consumption of what is served and a pressure-free interval for some digestion make a meal in France slow.<p>I&#x27;m also told that the coyness of the server in bringing the check comes from the feeling of community a restaurant strives to foster: &quot;we would rather you stayed here a bit&quot;. Getting pushy about turning a table over simply doesn&#x27;t happen in France.')
