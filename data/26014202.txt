Item(by='dangrover', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612364746, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26012006, text='This was what I thought when I was an angsty engineer in early 20&#x27;s, and I guess what motivated me to become a PM, where I realized it wasn&#x27;t true.<p>Doing the PM job well -- being rigorous about the product, its goals, its strategy, customers, how it interfaces with the external world, and figuring out how to distill that down internally to empower your team -- takes as much intellectual horsepower as engineering. I had to stop writing code every day to realize how deeply complex and interesting the non-code world can actually be. A good example is the passage in &quot;Superpumped&quot; about how Travis Kalanik validated the idea for Uber with deep research. It&#x27;s not just bullshitting through meetings.<p>I think the issue though is that, unlike eng, there is no obvious intellectual &quot;floor&quot; for PMs (equivalent of fizzbuzz for programming), so you&#x27;ve probably dealt with lots of bad, lazy, incapable ones.')
