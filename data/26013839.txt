He made his money at the moment you did the trade, because you traded at a disadvantage relative to the fair value of the product.<p>&gt; And, to run an option portfolio, professional options traders run risk management software like Blackrock Aladdin and Goldman Sachs SecDB. These cost a LOT of money (close to 6 digits a year)<p>No, options desks run (usually proprietary) models whose job it is to price options and strategies along the curve in a self-consistent way (i.e. via no-arbitrage arguments) relative to some more liquid instrument like futures.\nOR on a bank exotics desk, they run custom models that try to price exotic products from first principles (monte-carlo simulations of cash flows, basically.)<p>&gt; (close to 6 digits a year)<p>Way more expensive than this, the quants and developers who build these proprietary models are paid a lot of money.<p>&gt; Sure, you could get lucky once in a while but in the long run, not knowing the odds is going to hurt you and you are likely to lose all your money.<p>This is both accurate and not, it depends what you are doing. I believe sophisticated retail traders <i>can</i> find mispricings, especially in less liquid products.  If you are not looking at the vols and building curves, (or you don&#x27;t know what that means), this is not you.')
