Item(by='BeetleB', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612425129, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26023277, text='Fair enough. A key difference, of course, is that the commission is well defined compared to the tip, which is left to the whims of the customer.<p>Getting back to my original question: I often get better service from grocery store workers (depending on the chain, of course), than I do from restaurant workers. Yet no one pays them a tip. Same goes for Costco employees. Often the worker at the REI store will spend an inordinate amount of time helping me. No one pays them a tip, and they don&#x27;t get a commission. From my perspective, all of these people are actually providing a service to me that I actually want, when I want it. Whereas with waiters, they are typically forced upon me. It&#x27;s usually the exception that I want some service that requires a waiter.<p>The point being: There are more ways than tips to build a culture of good service, and it&#x27;s inane to think tips are needed to maintain quality.')
