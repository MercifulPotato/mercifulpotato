Item(by='jlmorton', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612329037, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26010709, text='It&#x27;s not like Russia embarked on a massive project of vaccinating 150 million people in November.  The manufacturing capacity was not yet there.<p>The vaccine underwent Phase I and II safety trials, and was thought to be safe.  Russia than began directing the limited quantities being manufactured towards at-risk health care workers, while they ran efficacy trials.<p>There&#x27;s nothing magical, or ordained by God that says we must run an efficacy trial with 30,000 individuals.  By early January, Russia had only administered a couple hundred thousand doses.<p>In retrospect, it&#x27;s highly likely this would have been the correct approach in all vaccines so far in use.  In the middle of a pandemic, there&#x27;s a very strong argument that should be vaccinating as quickly as possible after Phase II trials are complete, before waiting for a long-running efficacy trial.  As you said, efficacy trials are not testing for safety.  There&#x27;s already a huge risk to individuals and to society from the virus itself.')
