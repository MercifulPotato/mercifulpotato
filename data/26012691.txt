Item(by='Garlef', descendants=None, kids=[26015522], score=None, time=1612351885, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26012361, text='&gt; The whole thing is just about arrow composition!<p>There&#x27;s much much more to it.<p>For example, a version of the yoneda lemma also holds for metric spaces (instead of a set of arrows between to things, you simply have a number indicating a <i>distance</i> between two things).<p>Here&#x27;s how I like to think about the yoneda lemma:<p>If you have some kind of objects you want to talk about, one way to do this is by relating these objects to each other. Once you have established such a &quot;method of discourse&quot; (i.e. a way to talk about how your objects relate to each other) the yoneda lemma tells you:<p>1. You can forget about the inner structure of your objects; Everything is already contained in your &quot;method of discourse&quot;.<p>2. Inversely: The choice of a &quot;method of discourse&quot; severely limits what you can say about your objects.<p>3. There is no spoon: To understand how you can escape these limitations you need a &quot;method of discourse&quot; for &quot;methods of discourse&quot;.')
