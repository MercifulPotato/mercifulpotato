Item(by='jfengel', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612376406, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26015273, text='The article gives only a passing, buried mention to the thing I consider the most important part. It cites &quot;34 percent of Americans said they did not trust the outcome of the 2020 election&quot;, but it&#x27;s 45% of Republicans, outnumbering the 43% who oppose it. There&#x27;s also 12% who claim to be unaware, which seems highly unlikely.<p>If they are receiving official support, at least tacitly, that this was OK, then nothing anybody else says or does is going to matter. If the party can&#x27;t take a strong position that this is a bad position to hold, for fear of offending a near-majority of the party, what can we possibly hope to achieve as partners? You&#x27;d have thought that &quot;it&#x27;s bad to violently interrupt the peaceful transition of power&quot; was the one thing we could agree on.<p>In the absence of a partner in government, the other party must do what it thinks is best for the country, and hope that that&#x27;s positive enough to change some minds. But if they&#x27;re being encouraged to think of them as somebody who must be opposed violently, that seems unlikely.<p>To make a path back to society, we need a society for them to come back to. If half the country insists that there isn&#x27;t one, then there isn&#x27;t.')
