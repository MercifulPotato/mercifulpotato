Item(by='lmm', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612356305, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26011287, text='&gt; Jupiter has a number of characteristics that we can observe, such as gravity, that are best described by the theory of being a planet, heavily simplified. edit and if we remove Jupiter from our theory, we have a ton of observational data that now does not fit our theory.<p>Sure, but isn&#x27;t it the same for multiverse theories? Obviously we don&#x27;t observe the multiverse directly, but we observe data for which the best explanation also implies a multiverse, just as the best explanation for various observations implies the planet Jupiter.<p>&gt; Another universe, basically by definition, has no observable characteristics. We can seemingly only observe our universe, and have no way of knowing if something unobservable is impacting our universe.<p>There&#x27;s a lot we can&#x27;t observe or interact with directly; we can&#x27;t interact with the past, we can&#x27;t observe the future, we have to real way of interacting with distant galaxies, we can&#x27;t actually observe inflation at all... a good multiverse theory (of which I&#x27;m not sure there are any, but I think they&#x27;re possible in principle) would be in much the same category as inflation AFAICS: we can&#x27;t observe the thing itself but we can observe things that seem most likely to be consequences of it.')
