Item(by='johbjo', descendants=None, kids=[26003003, 26002615], score=None, time=1612281139, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25984996, text='The &quot;more expensive&quot; goods and services are not relocatable, or otherwise have special limitations on supply. College textbooks, for example, are priced monopolistically to maximize revenue as opposed to quantity. Housing is priced based on mortgage rates.<p>Improved manufacturing and (essentially) cheap Chinese labour is responsible for the cheaper goods.<p>The connection here is that &quot;inflation&quot; as it is measured by central banks is artificially lowered by improved and cheap manufacturing. This allows them to keep very low interest rates while being congruent with their &quot;charter&quot; of keeping stable average prices.<p>Artificially low interest rates can be seen as the cause of the most of the &quot;more expensive&quot; goods. It inflates the price of assets relative to income.<p>And there are many stake holders in asset inflation.')
