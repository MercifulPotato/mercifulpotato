Item(by='Layke1123', descendants=None, kids=[26009014], score=None, time=1612307517, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26007632, text='Claiming a different definition of free will does not make you right about free will existing.<p>At the fundamental levels of reality, free will does not exist. It isn&#x27;t even meaningful to talk about. Particles react to their environment. That&#x27;s it.<p>You are just a giant collection of particles reacting to it&#x27;s environment. You will never be able to be anything other than a giant collection of particles reacting to it&#x27;s environment.<p>Moving this collection of particles from one area that is unpleasant, say a really cold environment, to one that is warm is not an exercise in free will. It is largely a predictable process based on a sequence of events occurring within the collection of particles.<p>Defining free will as &quot;making choices&quot;, such as building a fire to warm your house because you are cold is not proving free will exists. It intentionally lies about the fundamental nature of reality that you don&#x27;t have to. Yes we make choices as far as we can tell from our experience of reality. But that doesn&#x27;t disconnect you from the underlying reality that it is not up to you what your particles do.')
