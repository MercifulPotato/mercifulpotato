Item(by='jeroenhd', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612302281, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26005793, text='I&#x27;ve used BTRFS on my laptop for a while and it was working fine. In my use case, however, I ran into trouble because I was constantly near the edge of filling the file system so I very often needed to do rebalancing and other tricks to claim more free space. It was a bother, but it beat spending $150 I didn&#x27;t have on a 512GB SSD at the time.<p>For modern systems with normal use cases (i.e. not mine back then), I don&#x27;t foresee a lot trouble. I went with encrypted ext4 because that was included in the installer during my reinstall, but I wouldn&#x27;t change the settings if the installer had defaulted to BTRFS.<p>There&#x27;s a lot more tooling out there for debugging ext4, but honestly, I never really missed anything when I still used BTRFS. The lack of tools makes data recovery harder in case of a complete failure (always make backups, no matter what file system!) so I wouldn&#x27;t use it in a production server, but for a desktop or laptop OS I don&#x27;t really see the problem.<p>Using Timeshift for snapshot management as a way to sort-of emulate Windows&#x27; system restore is kind of neat and got me interested in trying it out again next time I&#x27;m reinstalling Linux. Last time I didn&#x27;t have the space for snapshots, but now I do and it solves a problem I&#x27;ve had before (quick and easy backups after modifying system config) with ext4-based installs.')
