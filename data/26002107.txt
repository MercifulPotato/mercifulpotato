Item(by='mumblemumble', descendants=None, kids=[26003081], score=None, time=1612279993, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000768, text='One theory is that it&#x27;s a 15th century hoax. That fits the timeline reasonably well.<p>That strikes me as being more plausible than the idea that it was a later hoax. Depending on how much of the provenance you believe, that would place its creation sometime between the early 17th and late 19th centuries. That does stretch credibility a bit. The hoaxer wouldn&#x27;t just have to find some old calfskins; they would have to find an entire book&#x27;s worth of unused (I believe it&#x27;s been confirmed that the book is not a palimpsest) calfskins that all date to around the same time. And then they&#x27;d need to get all the other materials right, which is something that, depending on where in the timeline you are, may have required knowledge that was not available at the time.<p>The main problem that I&#x27;m aware of with it being a 15th century hoax is that it&#x27;s hard to imagine why someone would do such a thing. But this was around about the renaissance, where there was a revival in interest in obscure and ancient texts, and rich people who weren&#x27;t monks were starting to get interested in books. So perhaps there was something of an underground market for these sorts of things, which would at least permit a financial motive.')
