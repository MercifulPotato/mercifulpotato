Item(by='Juliate', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612444672, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26024806, text='Yes organizations are vastly more complicated, in a way they&#x27;re some kind of chaos, like a garden.<p>A garden, being big or small, is still chaos: not that there&#x27;s no order, but that there&#x27;s different species (animal, vegetal, mineral) that coexist and upon which coexistence you can plan some sort of order.<p>But that plan and that order is purely the product of both what you, as a gardener&#x2F;manager have in mind, and what the species can and let you do with them.<p>If you don&#x27;t have a good understanding of what you can or cannot do with what you have (the framework), and what level of engagement is required from you (let it flow or actively intervene&#x2F;change&#x2F;break rules). And if you don&#x27;t know people that are experts&#x2F;practitioners as you are, that may help you - you may by chance have something nice&#x2F;according to plan. Or not at all.<p>Well, music&#x2F;craft&#x2F;painting can also be (and is often) the result of a complex organization&#x2F;network, when you take all the elements in place to obtain something: you, your skills, your desire, your tools (and those that build them and can help you), your fellow artists.<p>What Bill&#x27;s talking about sound like a particular perspective on the Donning-Kruger effect, that applies to both small scale (managing single-person projects) and large scale (managing at large in an org).')
