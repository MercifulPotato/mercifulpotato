Item(by='derbOac', descendants=None, kids=[26024091, 26022299, 26023690], score=None, time=1612398729, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26018448, text='It sounds funny to say this, but sometimes I wonder if the obstacles to decentralization are simpler than it seems. Basically, it just requires the &quot;always on appliance model&quot; of home computers.<p>I&#x27;ve installed Briar. I like the decentralized aspect. It&#x27;s certainly not harder to use than Whatsapp or Signal or whatever. <i>However</i>, it requires me to have it running in the background all the time, and requires me to have my phone on. This is a power drain on my phone and sort of a limitation relative to some central server that&#x27;s always there.<p>The closest things to this are maybe a router or Alexa or whatever. My guess is if you could expand this to personal servers, like a nextcloud server-type thing in each home that you could expand, decentralization might go somewhere. People get this already, as they put them in their home for IoT, or to stream whatever, etc and so forth. You just get them used to these things being outward facing as well.<p>I think there will always be a need for centralized computing services, or at least a demand. But decentralization isn&#x27;t too complicated technically or in terms of UX, it just requires a sort of cultural shift, which isn&#x27;t really that different from use of Facebook or Twitter. It&#x27;s just a cultural shift on the hardware side.<p>Federation and decentralization was a central part of early home computing. There&#x27;s certainly reasons we moved away from it, but probably also reasons to return to it again.')
