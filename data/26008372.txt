Item(by='jfengel', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612307592, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25989645, text='I don&#x27;t see anything wrong with accounting for substitutions. If beef is too expensive this month, then people <i>do</i> switch to chicken. They haven&#x27;t lost anything.<p>It would be false if somehow this became an infinite treadmill where the basket went from beef to chicken to lentils to sawdust, but that&#x27;s not what happens. The substition smooths out short term changes in price the same way people actually do.<p>The economy acquires robustness through this redundancy, and the CPI measure reflects that. It&#x27;s far from a perfect measure, but its failures aren&#x27;t caused by the perfectly commonplace action of buying the cheapest goods <i>today</i>. Insisting that it has to be precisely the same goods would indeed be a distorted measure of the economy.')
