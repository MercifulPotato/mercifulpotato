We were in the fog of war and didn&#x27;t know how serious it was, so we assumed it was very dangerous. We did our best to avoid people and do our part to &quot;stop the spread&quot; or &quot;flatten the curve&quot;.<p>At the beginning of June, I started to get more bold with things. Wife and kids still avoided stores, but I would go for more than just the weekly groceries.<p>Sometime in July &#x2F; August we started seeing some of our friends again.<p>September &#x2F; October we started going to stores as a family again. Also started going to church again.<p>At this point, our habits are changed to the point that we just don&#x27;t really go anywhere, but we don&#x27;t avoid anything for &quot;safety&quot; reasons. We wear our masks in stores, etc. because most have signs on the doors asking us to and we try to be polite about it.<p>At this point I think I can count 20+ people of all ages that I know personally who have had Coronavirus. I don&#x27;t have a terribly big social circle. In the words of my best friend, whose family had it over Christmas, &quot;I wouldn&#x27;t recommend going out and catching it on purpose, but you get through it just fine&quot;<p>Which is to say, I think we know much more about the virus now than we did then, and the risk profile is <i>very</i> low for people without serious pre-existing health conditions or the very old.<p>And hell, my grandma&#x27;s nursing home had an outbreak. I think they had maybe 6 or 7 people die out of the 60 or so people who caught it.
