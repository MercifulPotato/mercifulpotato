Item(by='wrp', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612429680, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26021475, text='This article is based on his book Uncertainty (2016), which is a collection of various arguments about probability and statistics. Some of his arguments in the book are fairly well presented and some of them I agree with (not perfectly overlapping sets).<p>His view of probability is sort of a combination of objective Bayesianism and logical probability. He argues that all probability is conditional on some evidence, so it is meaningless to talk of the probability of a proposition in isolation from others. Further, since probability analysis is based on relations between propositions, it is actually a part of logic.<p>He belongs to the group who want to see hypothesis testing banished from statistics. The vast literature on that issue is easy to find.<p>The big change he wants to see in statistics is to follow a more machine learning approach. Develop models against a sample set, then test them against a testing set rather than against some ideal distribution.')
