Item(by='jbullock35', descendants=None, kids=[26016969, 26017337], score=None, time=1612372671, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26014616, text='The blog post is mainly about the difficulty of producing vaccines. I don&#x27;t see much in it that is objectionable. But this line may be misleading:<p><i>So no, we did not “have the vaccine” in February.</i><p>The author is arguing that, because it&#x27;s difficult to produce of the Moderna vaccine, we couldn&#x27;t have disseminated it on a wide scale in February or shortly thereafter. That argument may be fine, but it diverts attention from the critical role that the U.S. regulations in general, and the U.S. FDA in particular, played in slowing the process of widespread vaccine dissemination.<p>Specifically: the process could have been sped by months if the U.S. had been willing to conduct human-challenge trials [1] and to integrate tests of safety (Phase 2) and efficacy (Phase 3) [2].<p>To my knowledge, understanding of how to integrate the safety and efficacy trials has only recently developed; perhaps the FDA shouldn&#x27;t be faulted on that front. But the benefits of human-challenge trials have long been understood.<p>[1] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;doi.org&#x2F;10.1093&#x2F;infdis&#x2F;jiaa152" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;doi.org&#x2F;10.1093&#x2F;infdis&#x2F;jiaa152</a><p>[2] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;doi.org&#x2F;10.1198&#x2F;016214504000001790" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;doi.org&#x2F;10.1198&#x2F;016214504000001790</a>')
