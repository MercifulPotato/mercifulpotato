Item(by='wombatmobile', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612241841, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25996534, text='&gt; An ignorant person could believe that American ideals mean that American society was built for all Americans equally, but the audience members were not ignorant people.<p>I imagine most of them knew that Jefferson was a slave owner, and all the rest of it that you lucidly describe.<p>Baldwin did three things to earn their respect.<p>Firstly, he lured them into seeing racial injustice without triggering their defences or tribal hostilities, by giving them the vantage point of 4 year old eyes.  He told them his first 4 years of American acculturation was a positive experience, only spoiled when he realised the experience excluded him for an arbitrary reason.  They could feel his disappointment, and root for the underdog instead of having to parry tribal hostilities.<p>Secondly, he re-positioned his nemesis on the side of his cause, by revealing that a mind poisoned with racism is less free than a body that is steeled to fight it over recurring generations.<p>Then, after demonstrating his credentials as a high functioning elite peer through his mastery of history, oratory debating, and cultural diplomacy, Baldwin gave the Cambridge Union a realpolitik proposition they couldn&#x27;t resist.  If America is going to keep suppressing the rights of 1&#x2F;9th of its population, a new generation descended from slaves, led by the likes of himself and MLK, are going to educate the masses to blow the place up.<p>Baldwin did this so skilfully, no ultimatum was spoken.  He just shared his concerns.  The audience applauded because they shared his concerns through the osmosis he engendered by respecting the format of their debating society, and excelling at it.  As you point out, Buckley was unable to respond in kind.')
