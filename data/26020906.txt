Item(by='x86ARMsRace', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612396839, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26017997, text='You&#x27;re not missing anything. I&#x27;ve developed a prototype blockchain for a supply chain before at the behest of a non-technical manager at some point. You hit the nail on the head with the problem of crossing into the real world. You can&#x27;t validate the real world object mathematically, so if you lie to the blockchain, there&#x27;s no way to tell.<p>So far the only use case that I can really find for this are digital identity systems where the blockchain itself serves a double purpose as both a highly redundant system, and a byzantine fault tolerant distributed storage system. I can see the value in being able to maintain a strong ledger of ID uses. The EU looked into this from what I understand, and I think they stood up a system for this kind of thing.<p>My big TLDR from working on chaincode and stuff like that is this: if your goal is to have a high security digital asset (eg: tokens and ID certs), then a blockchain <i>might</i> be useful. If your goal is to interface with the real world, then you&#x27;re just going to be chaining yourself to a bulky inelegant solution that won&#x27;t even address your core problem.')
