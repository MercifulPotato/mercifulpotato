Item(by='ChuckMcM', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612391845, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26019769, text='As we return to &quot;browsers with an editorial opinion&quot; market differentiation is based on that opinion. Apple has been pushing privacy really strongly to move people to the Apple ecosystem. As the &quot;browser that won&#x27;t let you be tracked or compromise your privacy&quot; they have an opportunity to further &quot;choke&quot; the data&#x2F;advertising streams of competitors.<p>It is the advantage of having a weapon that you can use that damages your competitor but not you. Chrome started out that way[1] as a way to attack Microsoft (and to some extent Firefox)<p>When you see things like Tim Cook saying he will &quot;end&quot; Facebook, you might reasonably ask, &quot;Wait how could he possibly do that? He doesn&#x27;t control the web.&quot; And you would be right, with the exception of understanding that Facebook makes its money on data extraction[2], and a lot of Facebook users are also Apple device users. So on the Apple device Apple can build a browser that lets their users access Facebook while taking away as much data as possible from Facebook. This damages Facebook revenue, forces them to invest in counter measures, and doesn&#x27;t change Apple&#x27;s revenue model hardly at all.<p>As a result, from a business perspective, I think Apple could justify investment here in a browser that puts forward a point of view that makes it harder for Google and Facebook to make money.<p>[1] FWIW I worked at Google when Chrome was launched and understand that there was the &#x27;open standards&#x27; narrative but there was also a pretty obvious &#x27;hurts Microsoft&#x2F;Apple&#x27; narrative. So the technical reasons to invest aside, my opinion is that the real reason any large enterprise invests in anything is because they have a reason to believe it will make their business stronger, more difficult to attack, more profitable, or with luck all three.<p>[2] I think the term &#x27;surveillance capital&#x27; a bit too inflammatory.')
