Item(by='derbOac', descendants=None, kids=[26023421], score=None, time=1612416942, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26021475, text='I admit I&#x27;m confused about what the author is arguing.<p>They seem to be arguing that we should just adopt a strict causal predictive approach to data?<p>I&#x27;m not sure what that really means though. They seem to be just assuming that existing observations <i>are</i> the population?<p>They seem to be missing that every single estimation is a form of prediction. For example, if you estimate a regression slope parameter, you&#x27;re making a prediction about the degree and nature of association of two variables in future samples. The parameter is a random variable over possible observation samples. That parameter estimate, together with the associated model (e.g., bivariate normal observations) can be translated into a specific bivariate probability distribution prediction.<p>Lots of interesting issues are discussed but I&#x27;m not really sure they understand what they&#x27;re advocating for.<p>Statistics is always about prediction at some level, or can be formulated that way. Bayesians believe that data-based updates to models (which are used to predict) should be influenced by information available prior to the data; frequentists do not.')
