Item(by='myfonj', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612283848, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000405, text='His proposed solution  -- measuring screen size in <i>physical</i> display points -- is futile.<p>He writes it directly in his article:<p>&gt; My monitor’s native resolution is [w × h device pixels]. But I find the fonts slightly too small at that resolution – <i>given how far I sit from the screen</i>. [So I use DPI scale factor R, what] translates to an effective screen resolution of [w&#x2F;R × h&#x2F;R virtual (CSS) pixels].<p>(In his case it is 1080 × 1920 &#x2F; 1.5  = 720 × 1280).  Measuring screen dimensions in physical display points, like<p><pre><code>    var dppx = window.devicePixelRatio;\n    var screenWidth  = screen.width  * dppx;\n    var screenHeight = screen.height * dppx;\n</code></pre>\nreveals nothing about your preferences and conditions.<p>The moment he decides to get another second vertical monitor and put both <i>just a bit farther</i> he will be back on the square one.<p>You may have super tiny device with super high density display close to your nose, look at a screen on opposite side of your room that can have same density (what would be a bit wasting resources, but anyway) or look at super large billboard  screen across the road.<p>Until the page will know exactly:\n- how far is the screen from eye of its observer,\n- observer&#x27;s eyesight conditions,\n- and observer&#x27;s perceived visual size preferences,\nthere will be no way to solve virtual pixel problem the way that will make everyone happy.<p>(And that&#x27;s quite OK, I think.)')
