Item(by='pdonis', descendants=None, kids=[26012987], score=None, time=1612331393, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26010596, text='<i>&gt; How can you claim things that happen inside the human brain have causal influences on things outside the brain and yet at the same time ignore the causal influences the outside world has on the internal brain processes?</i><p>I have done no such thing. Obviously the causal influences go both ways.<p><i>&gt; What you seem to not grasp is the idea that external events can influence your behavior.</i><p>What you seem not to grasp is that one of the key roles that causal processes inside a person&#x27;s brain play is to control <i>how</i> external events influence the person&#x27;s behavior. The brain is not just a big switchboard where input A always leads to output B. Of course, vastly oversimplifying what actually happens in people&#x27;s brains in order to avoid having to question one&#x27;s theoretical model is a common mistake, going back at least to B. F. Skinner.')
