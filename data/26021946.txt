Regardless of how you feel about Uber&#x2F;Lyft drivers&#x27; ability to access benefits, if the bill that does it has this many exemptions, you should oppose it on that basis alone.<p>The long exemption list is a naked method of making sure that AB5 only targets its intended victims — Uber and Lyft, without actually having to mention them by name. If this law is generally a good idea, then why should it have required any exemptions at all?<p>The ethical version of AB5 would have been to have California (and the broader US) move have a single payer healthcare system, paid for by taxes, as it already is for a huge portion (but not all) of the population. That way, everyone gets access to health care, and everyone pays for it.<p>But that&#x27;s hard to do, so California&#x27;s cynical lawmakers &quot;solve&quot; the problem by passing the buck in a much simpler way: pass regulation to target their scapegoats du jour (as an assemblyperson, I get _my_ healthcare from the state, but it&#x27;s _those guys_ who are bad! yeah!) with laser precision through special carveouts. Thus AB5 is born.')
