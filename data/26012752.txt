Casual users do have issue with X; see this thread recently on HN: <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=25970690" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=25970690</a>. Would you consider that normal for users to handle?<p>Of course not. Casual users having no issue with X is a lie.<p>The fact is, that some subsytems on Linux are pile of hacks piled upon something built on assumptions that are no longer true. X11 is one of such subsystems. It is a dead end. When you are stuck in the dead end, you can either back up and once you are out, continue in your ride, or keep being stuck in the dead end.<p>Wayland is backing out of the dead end; yes, it is short-term, high intensity pain, when you are not going forward, and even have to go back for a while. Piling new hacks on top of X11 is trying how to continue going forward without backing out of that dead-end. You will be stuck in that place for a long time, and ultimately you will move nowhere. That is long-term, low-intensity pain. Some people do not take the long term view (or do want to avoid high-intensity pain), being stuck in a place works for them (and chronic low-intensity pain is fine to them).<p>These two approaches still clash, it is this <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.youtube.com&#x2F;watch?v=ZTdUmlGxVo0" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.youtube.com&#x2F;watch?v=ZTdUmlGxVo0</a>, all over again, 10 years later.')
