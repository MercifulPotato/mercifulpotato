Item(by='stonogo', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612378112, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26017330, text='It doesn&#x27;t enforce anything.  People can just transcribe wrong things into blockchains, then have to append patches to the information.  Unless you can prove malfeasance nobody is going to lose a license over a mistake.  I think you&#x27;re vastly overestimating how much anyone would care just because blockchains are involved. Notary records are already &quot;forever,&quot; no computers required, and there&#x27;s nothing inherent to blockchains that causes people to type more carefully.<p>Plus there are lots of situations in real estate law (like ground rent where the freeholder is not identifiable) which don&#x27;t fit into cute little data structures.  This isn&#x27;t a problem with documentation being a rush job, it&#x27;s a problem with some of the records of ownership being hundreds of years old.  Seeing as how I would have to hire a special archivist just to access data on an 8&quot; floppy disk, I&#x27;m not confident that the blockchain is a long-term (i.e. centuries) solution to any of these problems.')
