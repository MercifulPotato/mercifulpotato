Item(by='ben509', descendants=None, kids=[26023490, 26023276, 26023475, 26023409], score=None, time=1612417723, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26022370, text='Tips are fundamentally broken. If you didn&#x27;t know about tips, and someone offered you a job and said, &quot;I&#x27;ll pay you crap wages, but you can ask the customers for more money. Don&#x27;t worry, they&#x27;ll pay or their friends won&#x27;t like them,&quot; you&#x27;d think it was a scam because it&#x27;s such an obviously terrible deal.<p>Tips are an awful way to incentivize good service.<p>I&#x27;m neither qualified to judge my server&#x27;s performance, and I&#x27;ve got very limited and potentially misleading information about what a reasonable expectation is. I&#x27;ve got no idea what small crises my server had to deal with, or how other customers are treating him.<p>And because tips are <i>everywhere</i> now, nobody knows the going rate for most tips. Obviously tourists have no idea how much to tip.<p>If I reduce the tip, the waiter is only finding out that I was unsatisfied after it&#x27;s too late to fix anything. But tipping up front makes the exercise entirely pointless.<p>And the moralizing of tips is stupid.<p>Many moral arguments paint people in the service industry as paupers or victims, but a basic level of respsect has to assume they&#x27;re competent professionals who chose this line of work.<p>A tip is not an act of generosity, either, you&#x27;re paying a fee for a service rendered. And that&#x27;s why, as a system, the morality of tipping is entirely backwards: it rewards people who rip off their servers.<p>The thing that really bothers me are the awful motivations behind tipping: being control freaks, conspicuous consumption and all the slimy business incentives to push their payroll onto their customers.')
