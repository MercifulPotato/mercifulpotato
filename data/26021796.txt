At least based off the classical definition, “an uncultivated, uninhabited, and inhospitable region.”, about 80% of Wyoming, most of Utah, and Montana at a minimum count. Some of that is federal wilderness, but the vast majority of it is not.<p>Even here in overdeveloped California, the majority of the Sierra Nevada and Northern Coastal range easily counts, as does the ranges in the Mojave.<p>Federal wilderness designation is an administrative one to apply certain rules to a portion of federally controlled land, not a classification of the remoteness of something or a statement of how untouched it is. Technically the moment you step off Yosemite valley floor you’re in federal wilderness - try to not get distracted by the 1500 tourists staring at you or the 10 giant busses idling in traffic 100 feet away.')
