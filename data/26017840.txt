Item(by='doitLP', descendants=None, kids=[26020673], score=None, time=1612379826, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26010416, text='<i>Efficacy</i> describes what happens in a carefully controlled study. Vaccine efficacy is the percent reduction in disease incidence in a test group of vaccinated subjects compared to an unvaccinated group under optimal conditions. Such subjects usually do not have any underlying medical illnesses.<p><i>Effectiveness</i> describes what happens in the real world. Some tend to use ‘efficacy’ as if it is synonymous with the concept of ‘effectiveness’. This misuse is even rampant amongst doctors, who should know better.<p>In fact, vaccine effectiveness is the ability of a vaccine to prevent outcomes of interest in the real world.<p>For example, the influenza vaccine has been in use since 1938. It is spectacular at causing test subjects to make antibodies to the influenza virus. Now take a look at flu vaccine actual effectiveness  (or lack thereof):<p>There are universal recommendations to give flu vaccine to the elderly starting at age 65. A 2020 study [1] on the influenza vaccine in the UK compared outcomes when persons aged 65 to 75 got their yearly flu vaccines, compared to those of the same age who didn’t receive it. They were all followed for 4 years.\nThe ones who got flu vaccine did not have any lower hospitalization rates or lower death rates (from all causes).<p>This study showed that flu vaccine may be <i>efficacious</i> in controlled studies, but in real life it is often not effective in reducing death or hospitalizations – the outcomes of interest.<p>[1] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov&#x2F;32120383&#x2F;" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov&#x2F;32120383&#x2F;</a>')
