Item(by='neop1x', descendants=None, kids=[26013168, 26012333], score=None, time=1612342459, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26009609, text='w3c never wanted to implement physical units [1], saying OS are not reporting screen DPI reliably, etc.<p>I understand they had to start with something and 20 years ago no one expected thre will ever be monitors with DPI &gt; 96 but I don&#x27;t understand why the situation hasn&#x27;t changed yet. Now that we have many physical screen sizes with vastly varying densities.<p>In my opinion, devices with a screen are like a sheet of paper. Imagine a tablet or Remarkable eink. You can print only that small font on such paper. If the font is too small, we can use a magnifying glass or, in case of electronic devices, clever scaling mode.<p>If that was implemented, buying a bigger monitor would actually mean we could fit more content, wider newspaper there.<p>The current situation is just a mess with designers just randomly guessing how it&#x27;s gonna look on most current devices and using media queries to somehow automagically solve it all. But it is not uncommon to see a website on a huge hi-DPI monitor in such a way that a heading takes 1&#x2F;4 of the screen height and there are empty unused paddings everywhere. I.e. if you buy a bigger monitor, everything just gets bigger, including most of fonts, unnecesarily. Sometimes all you  get is three columns of text instead of one. Sometimes. If media queries are set up reasonably well. But even then, on high-resolution screens there is sometimes big empty space on the left and right side because it is just &quot;highest-width&quot; media query and designers didn&#x27;t expect you would have so many pixels horizontally...<p>[1] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;w3c&#x2F;csswg-drafts&#x2F;issues&#x2F;614" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;w3c&#x2F;csswg-drafts&#x2F;issues&#x2F;614</a>')
