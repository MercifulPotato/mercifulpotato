Item(by='jasonkester', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612252322, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25994417, text='My solution was to move away from traditional 9-5 work.<p>First, I moved into contracting&#x2F;consulting, which meant I could work shorter gigs that let me run at full speed for a few months at a time, then down tools for a while and recover completely before doing it again.  Normally said recovery would involve the better part of a year traveling through interesting parts of the world, which was a pretty big upside in itself.<p>The second part was building a couple Software as a Service products that eventually combined to bring in the equivalent of a dev salary on their own, while not taking up a lot of time to maintain.  That let me pick and choose just the most fun sounding consulting gigs, and whether to simply spend a given year traveling or working on my surfing.<p>The cool thing about either of these routes is that they eliminate the whole &quot;ageism&quot; thing.  None of my customers have ever asked my age before signing up for a trial of the SaaS.  And none of the startups I&#x27;ve worked with have held my lack of inexperience against me when they bring me in to build something for them.  If anything, &quot;25 years of javascript experience&quot; and some scary tales from the document.all days just helps to justify a high bill rate.<p>I suppose if I&#x27;m honest, I can see where you&#x27;re coming from with regard to slowing down and finding it harder to tolerate the non-stop-imaginary-crisis-to-ship that you find in so many shops these days.  But I find that I can still burst hard for short periods of time and build things quick, so long as I have a good amount of downtime between gigs.  Contracting is perfect for that.')
