Item(by='dragontamer', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612357745, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26012874, text='I&#x27;ve spent some time really studying memory allocation. It seems to me that almost any high-performance application will take advantage of the fine-details of memory allocators to optimize themselves.<p>There&#x27;s something to be said about garbage collection as an actual strategy (!!). I know people think that garbage collection is slow, but... very smart people have put their minds on the memory problem.<p>A generational garbage collector is designed to tackle fragmentation head-on. Every &quot;generation&quot;, the garbage collector recompacts all the data, &quot;squeezing&quot; your fragmentation out of the memory area.<p>Because some data is &quot;longer lived&quot; than other data, the compacted data is placed into a new generational-pool, where it is left alone for the next collection. IE: All data that survived a collection at time T will be left alone at collect time T+1. Maybe at time T+2 will it be re-checked... and those that survive the check at T+2 will be left alone until T+6.<p>--------------<p>This generational strategy minimizes the copying &#x2F; moving of data around the fragmentation issue. Still though: moving &#x2F; copying data is the singular catch-all solution to fragmentation.')
