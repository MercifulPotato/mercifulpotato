Item(by='wander_homer', descendants=None, kids=[26005950], score=None, time=1612296180, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26002766, text='&gt; Flatpak runs applications in what’s nowadays referred to as container: the application can’t see any processes outside the container, can’t access any filesystem except for files you explicitly open with the application, and especially can’t edit your .bashrc.<p>That&#x27;s just false. Flatpak has the ability to restrict access to such resources, but it&#x27;s completely up to the author of a Flatpak application if they make use of it. Like my file manager and text editor are Flatpaks fetched from flathub.org and both applications have unrestricted access to my whole home directory by default, including my shell configuration -- even the Chromium Flatpak can do that by default.<p>Edit: I just counted, out of 30 installed Flatpaks from flathub.org 19 have full access to either my whole home directory or the host file system.')
