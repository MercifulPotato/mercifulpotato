Item(by='StillBored', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612297491, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26003294, text='As I&#x27;m sure your aware, those mechanisms aren&#x27;t ideal. There are performance, maintenance and various other problems with them. They exist because distro&#x27;s are compiled to the lowest acceptable common denominator and then libraries&#x2F;etc are swapped in as needed. For something like x86, this is an acceptable tradeoff because there is an expectation that a distro boots on a 25 year old computer as well as the latest amd&#x2F;intel offerings with a ton of new instructions.<p>That said, if you rebuild many apps with -march=native, -flto, etc there are frequently large performance benefits due to the compiler being able to selectively use things like AVX512&#x2F;whatever for random code sequences where calling out to a library function, or checking for feature existence at runtime wipes out much of the perf advantage.<p>A large part of the advantage of a new architecture would be avoiding all this crap. If it comes baked in from the start that isn&#x27;t a good start, considering what it will look like in a decade or two.')
