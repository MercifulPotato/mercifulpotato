Item(by='tialaramex', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612282649, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25997311, text='&gt; assuming Moore&#x27;s Law held, we would be doubling computing power every two years<p>So the correct realisation here is that Moore&#x27;s law (an observation by an engineer) doesn&#x27;t trump laws of physics.<p>The transistors Moore was talking about have to be made from something. When they&#x27;re made of a lump of material you can actually see under a microscope this feels both very real and as if it could be shrunk indefinitely. Just keep cutting that material in half!<p>But it can&#x27;t. Matter is made of atoms. If you double the number of transistors you must halve the number of atoms in each transistor. Today&#x27;s transistors have a few hundred atoms in them. Guess what happens when there&#x27;s one atom in each transistor and you try to halve that? There&#x27;s no such thing as &quot;half&quot; an atom, what you&#x27;ve got there isn&#x27;t an atom any more, and so what you&#x27;re making isn&#x27;t a transistor.<p>The argument that we will never need larger symmetric encryption isn&#x27;t based on ignorance of Moore&#x27;s law, it&#x27;s based on <i>knowledge</i> of the laws of physics. You can&#x27;t make a 256-bit AES cracker by &quot;just&quot; converting the entire planet into Computronium, that&#x27;s not enough compute power.')
