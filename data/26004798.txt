Item(by='cossatot', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612291187, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26001373, text='The use cases for many people have changed dramatically as well. I think if I was an audiophile, and used headphones primarily to lie on the bed smoking hash and listening to Steely Dan, I would share your concerns.<p>But I use headphones for two things: talking on the phone&#x2F;listening to music when I walk my dogs or to pick up my kid, and for meetings at work (I&#x27;m long-term remote).  Having cordless technology is nice for the former, where it replaced cheap wired earbuds that are always tangled in my pocket and never last for more than a year or so.  It&#x27;s critical for the second, so that I am not chained to my desk for the 1-2.5 hour meetings that I have multiple times a week.  And I have to talk with my use cases, not just listen.<p>The heirloom-quality audiophile headphones are simply not workable for my use case, and I am sure that I am not alone here.  And my father, who I am pretty sure <i>did</i> spend lots of time lying on the bed smoking hash and listening to Steely Dan, did not ever pass his headphones down to me.')
