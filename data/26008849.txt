Item(by='gnicholas', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612310664, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26001373, text='Fair point, though I&#x27;m not sure there are enough people who are wealthy enough to pay $550 for headphones to meaningfully contribute to e-waste.<p>Regular AirPods, which are not repairable AFAIK, are more affordable and widely used. But they are also very small, so even if people replaced them every 2-3 years, the amount of waste would be minuscule compared to their waste from takeout containers, food waste, and other refuse. I suppose you could also factor in the energy spent producing and shipping the units, but it&#x27;s not as if repairable headphones (all of which are much larger than regular AirPods) don&#x27;t also incur these costs when they are produced and repaired.<p>So while I agree that it would be great if we could have more repairable things, I&#x27;m not sure that this is an especially good example of a product that will meaningfully contribute to e-waste.')
