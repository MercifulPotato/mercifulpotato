About a decade ago they made the call to make it more difficult to embed Gecko outside of Firefox.<p>Dead links abound: <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;developer.mozilla.org&#x2F;en-US&#x2F;docs&#x2F;Mozilla&#x2F;Gecko&#x2F;Embedding_Mozilla" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;developer.mozilla.org&#x2F;en-US&#x2F;docs&#x2F;Mozilla&#x2F;Gecko&#x2F;Embed...</a><p>Scary warnings: <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;developer.mozilla.org&#x2F;en-US&#x2F;docs&#x2F;Mozilla&#x2F;Gecko&#x2F;Embedding_Mozilla&#x2F;FAQ&#x2F;Embedding_Gecko" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;developer.mozilla.org&#x2F;en-US&#x2F;docs&#x2F;Mozilla&#x2F;Gecko&#x2F;Embed...</a><p>This choice (under Gary Kovacs, IIRC) has effectively made it a poor choice for _anyone_ to build on top of Mozilla&#x27;s browser engine without forking the entirety of the mozilla tree. You&#x27;d need to have a very good business justification for attempting it.<p>Since Chrome is essentially a superset of Chromium, it&#x27;s been obvious how to build your own equivalent of Chrome (and first-party supported!). There is no &quot;Firefoxium&quot; version of Firefox, and I&#x27;d expect developers would need to go to great lengths to keep their code up-to-date on top of mozilla-central.')
