This &quot;win&quot; does not compensate the losses nor justify the strategy. The win here is &quot;the decision outcome has not been smashed by a possible downside.&quot;<p>There were rather a lot of people suggesting that doing an unproven vaccine strategy of getting the maximum number of people their first vaccine and a longer period between first and second that had not yet been trialed, at all as a thing that was risky. People said it in advance. Who were the credible experts who recommended it? What support did they give that it wasn&#x27;t a pretty big risk? If there was some and I missed it I&#x27;ll put my hand up and say I was wrong. As I recall there was some serious doubt about whether anyone would get benfit from vaccination at all. Again if it was &#x2F;known&#x2F; that it was likely to work that&#x27;s not a bad decision - but I haven&#x27;t seen that analysis. Have you? Please do link it if you have. I&#x27;m also not clear about what the upside for running that risk was. Are there better results for people contracting covid after only one jab? Do we have data? Or was that a flyer that it might turn out to be? Any links you have with an analysis of that is welcome.<p>The &quot;herd immunity&quot; strategy was also described as risky by most experts that I saw. I think the word I used when looking at it, in advance of the numbers coming in was &quot;insane,&quot; while being well aware there was at least a nonzero chance it could work better than the conservative approach. To say it has not come off is an understatement. If it did come off somehow due to dumb luck it would have been equally terrible decision making. It&#x27;s too big a risk to take, the downside is immense.<p>It is reasonable to criticise a risk-loving gambling strategy for a nation&#x27;s health whether it comes off or not. I criticisised it before and after and I criticisise it when it comes off and when it doesn&#x27;t. By contrast a considered and careful strategy is to be commended even if it turned out to be incorrect based on subsequently available facts.<p>This is literally gambling on uncertain outcomes with people&#x27;s lives and health. You want to do that as sensibly as you can. You want the analysis. You want it public. You&#x27;re paying the downside. Getting lucky to get results out of bad decisions does NOT make a decision a good one.<p>But hey, take the thing as a whole. Has it been good, covid management in the UK or not? It&#x27;s worthwhile analysing what when wrong and why and how that compared with other, differing strategies. Taiwan&#x27;s looked pretty good initially and continues to. South Korea and Japan looked to have pretty good strategies and continue to. New Zealand and Australia had very different strategies to the UK.<p>It&#x27;s entirely reasonable to criticise a gambling addict&#x27;s decision making when they bet the rent check and the bet wins.<p>I hope that makes the point clearer for you, please disagree if you like but it should be clear that hindsight has nothing whatever to do with the criticisim.')
