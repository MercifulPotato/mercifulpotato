Item(by='DiogenesKynikos', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612341209, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26009713, text='You have to distinguish between SETI (listening in for communications by intelligent civilizations) and more general searches for signs of life in the universe.<p>SETI doesn&#x27;t get much funding, because Congress blocked it in the 1990s. Career-wise, this is also an incredibly risky subject to pursue as a researcher, because the most likely result is nothing.<p>On the other hand, other research into life in the universe is booming. The search for &quot;biosignatures&quot; in exoplanet atmospheres is a major subject in astronomy. One of the main goals is NASA&#x27;s Mars rovers is to look for evidence of past habitability.<p>It would be great if SETI got more funding, but it&#x27;s not as if there&#x27;s no research into extraterrestrial life. There&#x27;s a huge amount of research that isn&#x27;t as flashy as SETI: atmospheric chemistry of exoplanets, the geology of Mars, etc.')
