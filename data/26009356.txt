Just because I’m on a wide (or big) screen doesn’t mean I’m browsing with a wide window! I’m the guy throwing off your resolution stats if you do analytics, I do all my non-phone computing at a desk with a 42” 4K screen @1x. As in:<p>- My available pixels suggest HiDPI, but that’s wrong<p>- My browser window is probably twice as tall, and half as wide, as you think<p>The worst I usually encounter <i>gratefully</i> is bad scroll position content disclosure (sites designed to show or transition content on scroll assume a certain height and don’t even check, or mistakenly assume I’m looking at a different header than I am).<p>I’ll say it’s actually hard to accommodate my use case. I built a site that uses scroll position to progressively present content and my own browser window was harder to accommodate than any other (at this time on a 27” monitor with the same pixel density before 4K&#x2F;5K was common). Regression testing the more common screen&#x2F;window choices was a huge slog. It’s almost definitely such a weird set of circumstances that almost no site would bother. I <i>don’t blame them</i>. It’s very challenging and very unlikely to be your visitor who needs it.')
