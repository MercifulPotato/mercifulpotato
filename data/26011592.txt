Item(by='KMag', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612339353, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26010679, text='&gt; There&#x27;s a reason why RH is accused of helping Citadel front run their own customers.<p>Are there serious accusations of front-running (a serious financial crime) and collusion to facilitate that crime?  Or are you referring to people with a poor understanding of the interactions here making complaints that really boil down to privacy concerns, not actual front-running?<p>I haven&#x27;t seen anyone post any evidence of actual front-running.<p>For why Citadel would pay for retail flow, if they&#x27;re not committing financial crimes, market making is essentially profiting from separating pricing signal from pricing noise.  If the prices are jumping around randomly, you make money by evening them out.  If the prices are moving in one direction because new information has become available, you lose money if you try and damp out that signal.  Empirically, retail flow has a lower signal-to-noise ratio than the market as a whole, so market making on just that flow is more profitable, even though Reg NMS[0] requires them to execute at prices better than available in the open market (price improvement over NBBO).<p>By paying for a high-noise channel and damping out some of that noise before it affects the market as a whole, Citadel makes more money, at the expense of other market makers who would normally have exposure to that noise.<p>Disclosure: I work in financial services, but have never worked for Citadel.<p>[0] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.investopedia.com&#x2F;terms&#x2F;r&#x2F;regulation-nms.asp" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.investopedia.com&#x2F;terms&#x2F;r&#x2F;regulation-nms.asp</a>')
