Item(by='gugagore', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612284740, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000145, text='This is likely not a useful explanation if you do not specifically know what a a Phase-Locked Loop is, but I want to say that it is yet another example of how negative feedback can give you an implementation for the inverse of a component.<p>In this instantiation of the concept, a frequency divider (dividing by a whole number) is something that&#x27;s easy to build: it&#x27;s a counter. For example, to divide by 100, count the input clock, when you increment from 49, wrap around to 0, and toggle the output.<p>Putting that divider in a negative feedback loop gives you a multiplier.<p>Another common instantiation: if you put a voltage divider (easy to build with a couple of resistors) in the feedback path of an operational amplifier and you get a voltage multiplier.<p>Another instantiation with op-amps: Put a capacitor in the feedback path, and you &quot;get&quot; an inductor. This one is also a big deal because, practically speaking, it&#x27;s easier to engineer close-to-ideal capacitors than it is inductors, which are heavy and lossy.<p>And if you really want to take a ride on this conceptual train, consider that it&#x27;s easy to square a number. Need the square root?
