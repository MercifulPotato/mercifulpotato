In some concepts it is closer to traditional languages (mutable data structures, multiple inheritance OO, tools to write low-level efficient code). It is more extendable than clojure (reader macros, symbol macros, CLOS and MOP). With quicklisp you can find most of the libraries you might need.<p>Clojure is much more different from traditional languages. It puts immutable maps and vectors up front and expects you to work with data directly. Why is that preferable is hard to explain in a short post, if you&#x27;re interested about the ideas I&#x27;d recommend watching Rich Hickey&#x27;s talks like Value of Values[1].<p>&gt; neither of these languages can help me much in finding a new job<p>I don&#x27;t know about CL but there are clojure shops hiring. Depending on your employer if the JVM is being used you might get a chance to use clojure as well (or CL through ABCL for that matter). It&#x27;s also used by some shops for the frontend. And for your own work you can often choose what to use. Both can be used as a scripting language (clojure through babashka[2]).<p>&gt; CL sounds like the logical choice because of SLIME<p>I&#x27;m not sure what you mean.<p>&gt; Clojure sounds like the choice to avoid because of the JVM and the fact that I already know Go that plays in the same domain...<p>I don&#x27;t get the JVM hate. Sure, every platform has its issues, but the JVM is a highly tuned virtual machine that has decades of development behind it. It is cross platform. It is used for high performance projects as well. It has libraries for everything.<p>[1] <a href="http:&#x2F;&#x2F;infoq.com&#x2F;presentations&#x2F;Value-Values" rel="nofollow">http:&#x2F;&#x2F;infoq.com&#x2F;presentations&#x2F;Value-Values</a>\n[2] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;babashka&#x2F;babashka" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;babashka&#x2F;babashka</a>')
