Item(by='lionhearted', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612388834, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000436, text='I wonder if the causality might run the other way — like, if you imagine a set of &quot;all possible commitment levels of sports fans&quot; you&#x27;d have hardcore committed... moderately committed... middle of the road... somewhat fairweather... complete bandwagonner.<p>Something like that, eh?<p>Losing might strip away the fairweather&#x2F;bandwagoner fans, leaving only the people with a natural predilection to commitment. Winning might attract more bandwagon&#x2F;fairweather fans.<p>You could probably test this by finding multiple teams that had around a 50% win percentage and finding fans that adopt them in early adulthood, like someone who goes to college in Indiana starting to root for the Indiana Pacers (basketball), etc.<p>If you build a sample of around 1000 people that adopted different middle-of-the-road teams, then followed them longitudinally, you could see whether they increase in commitment, drop out, or stay about the same with rising and falling fortunes of the team.<p>A lot of work, though. No doubt certain experiences shape perception of fandom, but I imagine it&#x27;s different populations — not just the same type of starting population being effected by different events.')
