Item(by='rstuart4133', descendants=None, kids=[26010056], score=None, time=1612303893, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25991804, text='I&#x27;ve come across people using &quot;best practice&quot; as the sole justification for doing something in software engineering, but thankfully it&#x27;s rare.<p>Where it&#x27;s not rare is medicine.  You regularly hear the term spat out like a weapon, particularly in big hospitals.<p>It&#x27;s a very poor way to justify something.  It&#x27;s a statement of the obvious: everyone agrees you should use the best or most efficient way of doing something.  What people don&#x27;t agree on is what that is.  So it&#x27;s &quot;we all agree we should be doing it the best way, and I say the best way is ...&quot;.  You get no indication of why it&#x27;s best practice, or how it&#x27;s been chosen, or who chose it.  Its more an appeal to authority.<p>There is another term in medicine that&#x27;s also bandied about frequently: &quot;evidence based medicine&quot;.  Unlike &quot;best practice&quot;, it assums the obvious: everyone agrees we should be using the best way.  It&#x27;s telling you how the best way way chosen: from the evidence.  More importantly it&#x27;s also giving you permission to improve on it: find your own evidence.  If you asked for to see evidence for &quot;best practice&quot;, it&#x27;s often seen as an attempt to undermine authority.  If you asked to see the evidence behind evidence based medicine, the reaction your likely to see is &quot;oh, you get it, here is what I have&quot;.<p>When I hear &quot;because it&#x27;s best practice&quot; or &quot;this is best practice&quot; now, I shudder.  It&#x27;s not an invitation to be part of a team working to produce the best outcome.  It&#x27;s telling you you&#x27;re a minion, and you&#x27;d better follow the rules.<p>But I think you are right in one sense, the reason we see it used far less in software development is we can often shoot something down with a few sentences of tightly reasoned logic.  Evidence is often easy to come by.  Medicine doesn&#x27;t have that luxury, the body is too complex to reason about.  It does have rigor and scientific analysis, but it takes a big studies and a lot of effort to gather.')
