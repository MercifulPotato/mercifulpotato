Here&#x27;s the `read` implementation, for instance:<p><a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;bminor&#x2F;glibc&#x2F;blob&#x2F;21c3f4b5368686ade28d90d8c7d79c4c95c72c1b&#x2F;sysdeps&#x2F;unix&#x2F;sysv&#x2F;linux&#x2F;read.c#L24" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;bminor&#x2F;glibc&#x2F;blob&#x2F;21c3f4b5368686ade28d90d...</a><p>All it does is delegate to the low-level syscall, with some extra handling around to handle async calls (which can be removed when compiling glibc yourself, but you&#x27;re probably not doing this).<p>Here&#x27;s clone:<p><a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;bminor&#x2F;glibc&#x2F;blob&#x2F;21c3f4b5368686ade28d90d8c7d79c4c95c72c1b&#x2F;sysdeps&#x2F;unix&#x2F;sysv&#x2F;linux&#x2F;x86_64&#x2F;clone.S#L50" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;bminor&#x2F;glibc&#x2F;blob&#x2F;21c3f4b5368686ade28d90d...</a><p>This one&#x27;s written in asm, and you can&#x27;t really simplify it all that much more.<p>All the functions that wrap the low-level syscalls are very hard to get wrong, really.
