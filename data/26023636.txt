Item(by='pfranz', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612426406, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26015041, text='I didn&#x27;t use Windows for 10-15 years. Coming back I was amazed at how often and how long updates took (and how aggressive they got with them). Every few weeks I would have to reboot for an update and it took awhile. For macOS it&#x27;s like 2-3 times a year. The major update is the only one that takes a significant amount of time.<p>I&#x27;ve had more bad updates with Windows when dabbling than using Linux&#x2F;macOS full-time--but I&#x27;m willing to chalk that up to personal experience. What was annoying was they removed shortcut keys to access &quot;safe mode&quot; or &quot;recovery mode.&quot; You now let Windows detect it had a failed boot (surprise, it didn&#x27;t in my case).<p>Linux has way more options for updating. Unless you&#x27;re updating the kernel you don&#x27;t have to reboot (I believe there are ways around even that). You install the updates when logged in, so it&#x27;s a normal reboot and you&#x27;re not waiting longer. For large installs we booted straight off the network (PXE) which means unless you hit an undiscovered hardware or weird hardware config issue, you can test offline or rollback 100s of machines with a reboot.')
