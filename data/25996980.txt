Item(by='dragonwriter', descendants=None, kids=[25999151, 25997142], score=None, time=1612234606, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25996488, text='&gt; As a native english speaker, &quot;for example&quot; and &quot;that is&quot; code as basically the same style of skip-over phrase: just there to lead into the important part.<p>As a native English speaker, seriously, WTF?<p>“That is” (for which two different latin-derived abbreviations are frequently used in English, <i>i.e.</i> and <i>viz.</i>) leads into an equivalent alternative to what precedes, while “for example” (for which <i>e.g.</i> is a latin-derived abbreviation) leads into an example intended to illustrate the broader category which precedes it.<p>They are not equivalent, nor are they “skip over phrases”.')
