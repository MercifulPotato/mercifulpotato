Which is in fact the case.  But of course they would try him anyway because doing end-runs around the text of the Constitution is standard operating procedure and has been since the founding.  Even the whole idea of the Supreme Court having the power to declare laws unconstitutional is found nowhere in the Constitution.  That was a power invented by the Supreme Court in Marbury v Madison.  There are many other examples.  On a strict textual reading of the Constitution, if the second amendment applies to an individual right to bear arms (which is debatable, but that&#x27;s what the Supreme Court has most recently held) there is no basis for denying individuals the right to have WMDs.  There is no basis for making slander and libel crimes under the First Amendment, but we do, and no one argues with it.  Likewise, if someone committed murder in the &quot;death zone&quot; they would be tried somewhere and convicted and the Supreme Court would simply refuse to hear the appeal. And that would be that.')
