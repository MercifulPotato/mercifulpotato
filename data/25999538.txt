Item(by='majewsky', descendants=None, kids=[26001017, 26001352], score=None, time=1612263542, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25999291, text='&gt; It&#x27;s the sort of thing that seems attractive to junior developers<p>You make it sound like Wayland has been pushed by some script kiddies, when in fact the lead developers on Wayland are the actual Xorg maintainers.<p>To me, the migration from X11 to Wayland looks similar to the switch from DOS-based Windows to Windows NT. At the time, lots of programs were broken by the move because they assumed you could meddle in other application&#x27;s internal memory. But breaking those applications was not malicious intent on the side of the Windows devs. It was a legimitate move towards a more sustainable architecture. I&#x27;m not intimately familiar with X11 internals, but my read of the situation is that X11 just exposes too many internals in its interfaces, and therefore it becomes increasingly impossible to adapt it to contemporary needs (in this case, application sandboxing).')
