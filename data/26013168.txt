Item(by='jefftk', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612356683, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26011845, text='The current situation is not a mess; it works pretty well:<p>* Manufacturers configure their devices to ship with reasonable default settings, where is CSS pixel is roughly the same apparent size for everyone.<p>* Users who want content to be displayed larger or smaller can change their device scaling, which then makes their CSS pixels larger or smaller.<p>* Web developers can write for CSS pixels, knowing the content will display at a reasonable size for the user.<p><i>&gt; if you buy a bigger monitor, everything just gets bigger, including most of fonts, unnecessarily</i><p>It sounds like you&#x27;re probably sitting closer to the monitor then expected? Lower your device scaling and things will be a better size in all programs including the web browser.<p><i>&gt; on high-resolution screens there is sometimes big empty space on the left and right side because it is just &quot;highest-width&quot; media query and designers didn&#x27;t expect you would have so many pixels horizontally</i><p>I think this is also running into user preferences and design.  If I&#x27;m reading an article, and there is just one stream of text, I&#x27;m quite happy for it to take a 40em strip down the middle of my screen.  If the text ran edge to edge it would be much harder to read, because it&#x27;s hard to keep your place when jumping to the next line.')
