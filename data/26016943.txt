Launch, expect discard of 100%* of launch system. Starship is designed for 100% reusable, with same-day fly&#x2F;land&#x2F;refuel&#x2F;repeat. That’s obviously a huge difference.<p>* - exceptions:<p>Space Shuttle discarded most of the launch system, acting as glider to return crew&#x2F;cargo and some engines. Reuse turnaround was months, with extensive repairs expected. Boosters required prolonged recovery and refurbishing, with high likelihood of loss.<p>Falcon 9 is only one remotely viable for same day return to pad and reuse of first stage. Second stage is discarded.<p>SN# is a fast-reuse second stage. Landing involves leveraging the terminal velocity physics of a brick (not gentle glide of Shuttle), followed by effectively flipping a 16-story building ~90° in free fall with powered deceleration to 0m&#x2F;s in less than 2km vertical.<p>Nothing in the industry resembles the fast launch&#x2F;fall&#x2F;relight&#x2F;reorientate&#x2F;decelerate&#x2F;land&#x2F;refuel&#x2F;repeat sequence, of SN#. There is no established industry specifications addressing such operation. And as the history of rocketry shows, establishing such specifications practically involves lots of crashes.
