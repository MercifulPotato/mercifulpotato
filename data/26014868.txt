Item(by='masklinn', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612368141, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26007149, text='&gt; With 8M&#x2F;4M&#x2F;2M&#x2F;1M&#x2F;512k stack size, 4K page size, you get about 800M&#x2F;800M&#x2F;800M&#x2F;600M&#x2F;500M RAM usage<p>Simulating this (by creating 100k maps of the relevant sizes) there is no difference in RES between 8M pages and 512K pages: it was ~495M for both, only the VMEM varied (respectively ~780G and ~50G). Touching the second page increased the RES of both to ~816M, which is about what you&#x27;d expect.<p>This is on a more or less stock x64 Mint.<p>&gt; I think main problems start when you actually touch more than the base allocation of the stack<p>The thing is you&#x27;re unlikely to do that in all of your 100k routines, most of them will not grow beyond their first page, and <i>maybe</i> their second… at which point the routine&#x27;s stack would have grown to 8k <i>anyway</i>.<p>&gt; maybe segmented stacks grow from 200M to 300M, 500M or whatever you actually use at a given time (with say 20-60% efficiency)<p>Go has not used segmented stacks since 1.3.<p>&gt; your C-compatible stacks might go from 500M to 3G¹ if you on average touch just 32K of stack per thread with some unlucky function<p>Your not-C-compatible stacks will do the exact same thing. Since 1.3, stacks are realloc&#x27;d and double in size on every overflow.<p>The Go runtime will also shrink stacks if able (halving them) during a GC run, but you can do essentially the same thing on your C stack using madvise(2), and without the need to copy stack data around.<p>So what gain there is, is really only for goroutines which never grow beyond their initial size (and only since the default stack was decreased from 8k to 2k in 1.4), at the cost of all the C incompatiblity mess. And it assumes these 2k stacks are allocated from a reusable pool (which is probably a fair assumption though I certainly did not check it) otherwise they&#x27;d be on different pages anyway.')
