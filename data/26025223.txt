Item(by='flohofwoe', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612446224, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26025063, text='Tagged unions have always been around as runtime constructs, they just had different names (e.g. &quot;variant&quot;). In C it&#x27;s understandable why they aren&#x27;t part of the language: they would be the only data type that requires a &quot;builtin&quot; opaque struct type to hold the tag and payload union (e.g. something like this, but hidden from the programmer):<p><pre><code>  struct my_tagged_union {\n      enum my_tag_enum tag;\n      union {\n        ....\n      } payload;\n  };\n</code></pre>\nThat would be a pretty big break with &quot;tradition&quot; for the C language ;) (e.g. it would be similar to a &quot;builtin string type&quot;)<p>And of course tagged unions as &quot;generic user-defined types&quot; are much less convenient&#x2F;useful than being directly integrated into the language syntax and type system.')
