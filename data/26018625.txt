Item(by='corysama', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612383791, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26018022, text='Can you be specific of the context you are interested in?<p>On modern machines, a pixel is just a set of three numbers indicating how much red, blue and green light should be shown at a particular point.  Ex: {0.75 red, 0.0 blue, 0.5 green} for a kinda-dark, orange pixel.  The GPU keeps a big 2D grid of these number-triples in memory and on a regular schedule sends out a copy over the DVI cable to your monitor.  The monitor has a bit of memory to hold it&#x27;s copy.  And, it has hardware to scan over the grid of numbers to produce a sequence of voltage levels that are used to change the color of the points on the LCD.<p>There&#x27;s a bit of math involved in how to do a good job representing colors with numbers and how to convert those numbers to voltages.  But, at the most basic level, an image is just a big 2D grid of numbers.  If you want to change the image, poke the grid.  People want to change images a whole lot.  So, we&#x27;ve developed pretty sophisticated hardware and software around poking 2D grid...  But, that&#x27;s a whole other topic.')
