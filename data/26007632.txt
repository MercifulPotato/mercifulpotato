Item(by='pdonis', descendants=None, kids=[26008353, 26008275], score=None, time=1612304032, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26007306, text='<i>&gt; Feel free to email me for further discussion.</i><p>Why not just have the discussion here?<p><i>&gt; I have the impression you don&#x27;t understand my definition of free will compared to your own definition.</i><p>I think you are trading on the ambiguity in the term &quot;free will&quot; to avoid having to confront the actual issues involved. That&#x27;s why I used the less ambiguous term &quot;making choices&quot;.<p>If you don&#x27;t think people can ever make choices that make a difference in their situation, then you and I have a fundamental disagreement that I don&#x27;t think will get resolved by any discussion. Also, if that&#x27;s your belief, I think you are being inconsistent; you talk about &quot;designing better social systems&quot;, but that very process involves people making choices that will make a difference in their situation (as well as the situation of many, many other people).<p>If you just think the <i>amount</i> of difference a person can make in their situation by making choices varies with the situation, of course I agree with that. But that&#x27;s not a problem that can be fixed by &quot;designing better social systems&quot;. It can only be fixed by being willing to call a spade a spade when people in power make choices that disempower others, so that people in power can be <i>stopped</i> from doing that. The biggest barrier to people being able to change their situation by making choices is restrictions put on them by other people, not some abstract claim about free will being an illusion. &quot;Designing social systems&quot; makes that problem worse, not better.<p><i>&gt; I fundamentally think it&#x27;s morally wrong to keep someone in the dark from reality by deceiving them about their will or life outcome</i><p>I think you are confusing your opinions with &quot;reality&quot;. Telling people they don&#x27;t have free will, or that free will is an illusion, is just as much of an opinion as telling them they <i>do</i> have free will. Neither is a statement of &quot;reality&quot;. That&#x27;s why I say free will is best viewed as a right: because in my opinion, believing that people have free will is respecting their right to make their own choices, and believing that people don&#x27;t have free will is <i>not</i> respecting that right--which just means arrogating to yourself the power to make choices that disempower them. Respecting people&#x27;s right to make choices is not a factual claim about people; it&#x27;s a policy, which I think should be adopted because it will end up helping people.<p><i>&gt; especially if that person is homeless or suicidal for example</i><p>I don&#x27;t see how it&#x27;s any help to a person who is homeless or suicidal to tell them free will is an illusion. Nor would it be any help to tell them it isn&#x27;t. A person who is homeless or suicidal has much more pressing things to think about than whether or not free will is an illusion. And helping such a person has nothing at all to do with your own opinions or beliefs, much less foisting them on others in the guise of &quot;telling them about reality&quot;.')
