Item(by='ineedasername', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612335316, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26011068, text='No, a union contract supersedes a &quot;right to work&quot;, because it&#x27;s a contract. Yes, an employer can fire you for little&#x2F;no reason (if it&#x27;s not discriminatory) but if they sign a contract saying they <i>won&#x27;t</i> fire you, then their hands are tied.<p>In the example of something like missing a day of work, the contract will spell out the specifics of advance notice for days off&#x2F;sick days. If it&#x27;s completely unannounced, the contract will spell out the steps of a disciplinary process.<p>Source: I work in a right to work state, used to be a union member, asked this very same question at the time.')
