Item(by='tannhaeuser', descendants=None, kids=[25999604, 26000365], score=None, time=1612262748, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25999218, text='I haven&#x27;t seen many desktop apps emerging in the last decade, so whom is that &quot;legacy is holding back the Linux desktop&quot; argument addressed at? X may not be the most modern architecture, but it&#x27;s still the API almost all F&#x2F;OSS desktop apps are ultimately written and tested against. For many apps, wayland doesn&#x27;t bring a new perspective, but end of life with no new apps taking their place. For other apps, wayland means additional testing and porting effort. And wayland, like gnome and almost every other &quot;innovation&quot; by RedHat, brings fragmentation into the free desktop world also for BSD and Mac OS ports. Desktop app development, being starved off developers anyway, doesn&#x27;t need a &quot;Python 2&quot; moment, that is, a decade+ long phase of transition in the name of dubious progress, or other impediments getting thrown in its way. The end result is simply that less usable software will exist.')
