Item(by='JumpCrisscross', descendants=None, kids=[26017604], score=None, time=1612378028, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26017056, text='&gt; <i>There&#x27;s a simple lesson. Buy and hold a low-fee index fund. Stop trying to pick stocks. And for God&#x27;s sake stop trying to day trade stocks.</i><p>It&#x27;s a tough lesson to learn. When I realized, I think last Wednesday evening, that intelligent people honestly believed Ken Griffin was ordering Robinhood to block traders from buying GameStop stock so a short of his could make money, I started pricing out puts. (Didn&#x27;t do anything.)<p>I burned my hands with thankfully little money in the &#x27;08 crisis. It chastened me, and I&#x27;ve been more disciplined since. And I was a professional options market maker. (It&#x27;s a bit of a running joke: sell-side traders running godawful personal accounts with a handful of hero trades and boatloads of losers.)<p>I&#x27;m not sure I would have been more disciplined had I entered the markets at the tail of a 10-year bull market. Certainly not if the most-accessible trading app encouraged me to day trade. And if my financial identity was largely based on a subreddit that egged me on. It&#x27;s a difficult problem, and I don&#x27;t think there are easy answers.<p>(Counterfactual: individual investing is not hopeless. There is ample evidence that individual investors <i>can</i> and <i>do</i> generate abnormal returns. But it&#x27;s seldom the people day trading, and when it is, it&#x27;s not them holding concentrated positions over multiple days.)')
