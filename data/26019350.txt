Item(by='throwaway894345', descendants=None, kids=[26019419, 26019564, 26019382], score=None, time=1612387663, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26019293, text='I don&#x27;t know why &quot;having generics&quot; is regarded as an end unto itself, or even a particularly important feature. The more experienced I&#x27;ve become as a developer, the more I realize that the things that matter tend to be tooling, ecosystem, learning curve, documentation, standards, performance, etc. The things that tend not to matter so much are type system particulars. If people can make crazy money with dynamically typed languages, having a type system that provides static guarantees for 95% of use cases (beyond which you have to opt into dynamic typing via interface{}) is honestly fine. It&#x27;s not <i>elegant</i>, but it gets the job done for a huge swath of software. Generics are nice to have, but I think people blow them wildly out of proportion.<p>EDIT: My implicit criterion for programming languages is the extent to which they facilitate software development, not some pursuit of elegance unto itself (although I fully empathize with the latter). These two things are not particularly congruent, but I often get the vibe that much criticism of Go assumes that it exists to be elegant instead of practical.')
