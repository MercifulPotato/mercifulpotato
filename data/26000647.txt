Item(by='pjc50', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612272247, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000145, text='PLLs (&quot;phase locked loop&quot;) are entirely normal in both FPGA and microcontroller worlds. They involve a high-frequency oscillator and a frequency divider. The fast clock is divided down, as is the fast one, and the two signals compared. When they are <i>out of phase</i>, one is ahead of the other, and the fast oscillator adjusted accordingly.<p>This is also how PC BIOSes let you configure various CPU and DRAM speeds from a single physical crystal.<p>They&#x27;re accurate enough for most practical purposes. They can take a large number of clock cycles to acquire &quot;lock&quot; and start producing the correct frequency, which rarely matters unless you need to go from 0 to X00MHz very quickly.<p>&gt; system clock doesnt run fast enough to keep up with 60hz<p>It doesn&#x27;t actually say that as far as I can tell?')
