Item(by='ulber', descendants=None, kids=[26023701, 26023107, 26021932], score=None, time=1612397770, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26018928, text='This post got me into looking up what Google&#x27;s privacy sandbox actually is. One proposal I found quite interesting was TURTLEDOVE, which proposes to move ad bidding onto the user device [1]. The current proposal of course uses bidding logic delivered by the ad seller. However, if this were to gain wide adoption, the step to imposing <i>user</i> control over bidding gets a bit smaller.<p>Imagine being able to tell advertisers &quot;ok I&#x27;ll look at any ad that pays me at least 50 cents&quot; (~100X the typical price of an impression currently). If anyone wants to show you an ad at that price you know they think its going to be relevant. That&#x27;s the kind of targeting I could get behind.<p>No chance that would actually happen though.<p>[1]: <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;WICG&#x2F;turtledove&#x2F;blob&#x2F;master&#x2F;FLEDGE.md" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;github.com&#x2F;WICG&#x2F;turtledove&#x2F;blob&#x2F;master&#x2F;FLEDGE.md</a>')
