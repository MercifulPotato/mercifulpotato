Item(by='brundolf', descendants=None, kids=[26018040, 26017703], score=None, time=1612377648, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26016011, text='The issue isn&#x27;t bandwidth; Netflix can stream HD video over a 5Mbps connection, and Stadia should require about the same bandwidth as video streaming because that&#x27;s essentially what it&#x27;s doing (and sending input signals back the other direction, but those should be negligible)<p>What&#x27;s much harder is latency. Who cares if your interactions with Netflix have a 500ms delay? For a video game, even 50ms is a problem. And you can&#x27;t improve latency just by adding bigger pipes: there are unshakeable physical limits around how long it takes an electrical signal to go to and from a data center X miles away, and on top of that there&#x27;s the rat&#x27;s nest of routing that the signal has to go through along the way. The former can&#x27;t be solved except by building out more datacenters so that more people are closer to them. The latter can be solved, at great effort, but I don&#x27;t see ISPs having much motivation to do so. This is why individual experiences with Stadia are so hit-or-miss: it mostly comes down to luck, in terms of a) how physically close you are to a datacenter and b) how streamlined the network infrastructure around you happens to be. And I don&#x27;t see a clear path for Google to significantly improve this over time.')
