Not even apples versus oranges.<p>Formally, `A + B` is isomorphic to `forall T. (A -&gt; T, B -&gt; T) -&gt; T`, where the inner `(A -&gt; T, B -&gt; T)` is the type of a visitor.<p><pre><code>    a + b\n    -- CPS transform\n    = forall t. ((a + b) -&gt; t) -&gt; t\n    -- distribute -&gt; over +\n    = forall t. (a -&gt; t, b -&gt; t) -&gt; t\n    -- if you want to drop even the product, distribute -&gt; over *\n    -- but this loses you the ability to name a separable Visitor\n    = forall t. (a -&gt; t) -&gt; (b -&gt; t) -&gt; t\n\n    type Visitor a b t = (a -&gt; t, b -&gt; t)\n    type Sum a b = forall t. Visitor a b t -&gt; t\n\n    iso :: Sum a b -&gt; (a + b)\n    iso s = s (Left, Right)\n    \n    osi :: (a + b) -&gt; Sum a b\n    osi (Left a) = \\(onA, onB) -&gt; onA a\n    osi (Right b) = \\(onA, onB) -&gt; onB b</code></pre>')
