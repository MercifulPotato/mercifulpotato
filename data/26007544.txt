Item(by='PaulHoule', descendants=None, kids=[26007914], score=None, time=1612303602, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26006957, text='Itanium&#x27;s problem I know pretty well.<p>The idea of scheduling parallelism in the compiler (VLIW) doesn&#x27;t work for mainstream workloads because the time it takes for data to come back from the DRAM is highly variable.<p>A super-scalar processor can possibly run some instructions at the hardware level while other block wait until data gets back from DRAM.<p>A VLIW processor packs N instructions together (say N=3) and if one of them is blocked by DRAM,  they all block.  (If one of them is blocked by Optane they all block for a very long time...)<p>It looks obvious in retrospect but it&#x27;s amazing how most of the RISC workstation vendors missed it and put themselves out of business by getting on the Itanic train.<p>(VLIW is successful for DSP and GPU,  but that&#x27;s because workloads like that can have completely predictable fetches)<p>I don&#x27;t know what the problem w&#x2F; Cell was exactly,  but it was the same in that it couldn&#x27;t pull data from DRAM fast enough to keep the silicon busy.')
