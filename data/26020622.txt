Killing 3rs party cookies isn&#x27;t anti-competitive, in fact I&#x27;d argue any decent browser should do it to protect their users&#x27; privacy.<p>What <i>is</i> possibly anti-competitive is their proposed alternative. And frankly I&#x27;m not sold on the supposed privacy of such an alternative either (which, in order to be functionally different from 3rd party cookies, will have to store personal identifying information in a location that users cannot access, edit and&#x2F;or control).<p>Note also that (to my knowledge) browsers are <i>not</i> currently obligated to protect their users&#x27; privacy (though perhaps they should be, it makes more sense than punishing websites for sending cookies). This leaves google at least 2 completely legitimate options:<p>1. Keep 3rd party cookies.<p>2. Kill 3rd party cookies.<p>Sure the first will cost them their privacy conscious users and the second might hurt their ad business, but <i>rejecting</i> that choice on the sole grounds that it would hurt the bottom line of a <i>different</i> venture is textbook anti-trust. They&#x27;ll probably list other reasons for not taking that second option, though I&#x27;ve not found them terribly convincing so far.')
