Item(by='jfengel', descendants=None, kids=[26003943], score=None, time=1612281640, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25963375, text='Using &quot;some&quot; is a pretty thin fig leaf. It may be technically correct, but it leaves an enormous amount of wiggle room such that the person can say &quot;I meant a few&quot; but people can hear &quot;practically all&quot;. It might be sufficient to tip a moderation judgment from &quot;racist&quot; to &quot;just barely not racist enough&quot;, but I don&#x27;t think it substantially alters what was said.<p>And that&#x27;s exactly what&#x27;s being discussed here: what people hear, regardless of the language. That&#x27;s precisely what &quot;white fragility&quot; is all about: the way some white men jump on every perceived slight. Because there are so many of us and we&#x27;re so relatively powerful, people hear us when we say it, when less favored groups are dismissed.<p>So when you say &quot;what if it were some other group&quot;, the fact is that we&#x27;re not some other group. When minorities say &quot;We demand equal rights&quot; and white men say &quot;Well, if you&#x27;re getting more rights, then so should we&quot;, you can understand why people get grumpy.<p>There isn&#x27;t an easy general solution. But in going about looking for a better one, it does help to listen to what people rather than just the surface meaning of the words. The surface meaning is often the least important.')
