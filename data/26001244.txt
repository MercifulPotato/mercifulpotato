Though masked somewhat by self-deprecating remarks, there&#x27;s audiophile &quot;woo&quot; peaking out from behind the corner (e.g. the comment about lossless compression). More importantly, it&#x27;s missing the most important part of a headphone review: a measurement of the frequency response curve!<p>It&#x27;s fairly well known that above the ~$100 price level, headphone sound quality differences mostly disappear once equalized properly [0]. The real test is not stock AirPods versus H9i (as done in the review), it&#x27;s AirPods versus H9i once they&#x27;re both equalized to a common frequency target. Otherwise you&#x27;re testing the manufacturer&#x27;s tuning as much as the actual quality of the hardware.<p>Certainly, there&#x27;s something to be said for the manufacturer getting it right in the first place so you don&#x27;t have to bother with EQ when listening on a mobile device, etc. But I think the following two points are likely true:<p>1) People will score the AirPods highly in subjective listening tests;<p>2) The performance in those listening tests has more to do with the frequency tuning of the AirPods than anything about its hardware, and you could get a virtually identical listening experience with a $100 to $200 pair of Sennheisers and an EQ app.<p>[0] See e.g. <a href="http:&#x2F;&#x2F;seanolive.blogspot.com&#x2F;2017&#x2F;02&#x2F;twirt-337-predicting-headphone-sound_17.html" rel="nofollow">http:&#x2F;&#x2F;seanolive.blogspot.com&#x2F;2017&#x2F;02&#x2F;twirt-337-predicting-h...</a>. Sean Olive has a bunch of papers on this.')
