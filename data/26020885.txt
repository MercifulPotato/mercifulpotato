Item(by='quikacct', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612396622, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26019792, text='This is an EXCELLENT comment. Learning not to engage with the anxiety is a really important skill. To get there, you&#x27;ve got to get in the habit of labeling those thoughts as anxiety so that you can recognize them easily.<p>And you&#x27;re right -- trying to evaluate how likely the feared event is can be a form of engaging with the anxiety. You can even start cultivating satellite fears about whether you&#x27;ve got the right read on the situation and so forth.<p>Nevertheless, I would caution against a blanket rule that says &quot;never touch an anxious thought.&quot; Sometimes, you need to make a decision. Let&#x27;s say you&#x27;re feeling feverish, and you think: &quot;maybe I have COVID-19!&quot; It is appropriate to fetch the thermometer from the medicine cabinet and check your temperature -- once. If the reading is normal, and you have the impulse to check again 30 minutes later, that would a good time to simply label the anxious thought and refrain from responding to it.')
