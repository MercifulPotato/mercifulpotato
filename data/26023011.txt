Item(by='vladvasiliu', descendants=None, kids=[26023050], score=None, time=1612417839, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26022858, text='&gt; The people who steal the difference between a living wage and a tip-subsidized wage?<p>The people who could &quot;steal the difference&quot; are usually the owners, right? Not sure why GP put the owners and kitchen staff in the same bucket.<p>From my experience as a client in bars (without actual kitchens) in Paris, France, where tipping &quot;isn&#x27;t expected but kinda is&quot; [0], most of the time tips are split between wait staff. So I kind of get the issue that if some waiter is going above and beyond and is nice &#x2F; helpful &#x2F; quick to serve &#x2F; etc, whereas a colleague will do the bare minimum, when someone tips, this is diluted.<p>As a client, I&#x27;m tipping X for the service, but this particular waiter will only get a fraction of that, the same as his low-effort colleague. I&#x27;m not really OK with that.<p>Most places I usually go are rather small and the waiters seem comparable, so I guess it&#x27;s not usually an issue I have in practice.<p>However, there are other, usually larger places where quality of service varies much more, and I&#x27;m usually much less inclined to tip in those places. I&#x27;ve also heard from bar staff having worked in such places that there indeed is more tension between staff, along the lines of &quot;so and so just waits around &#x2F; doesn&#x27;t do anything&quot;. Wouldn&#x27;t be surprised that the complainer wouldn&#x27;t be all too happy to split their tips in such a case.<p>---<p>[0] Technically, in France, prices are &quot;all-inclusive&quot;, that means service is already included in the price, so you don&#x27;t <i>have</i> to tip. However, at least in bars around Paris, I&#x27;ve noticed that it&#x27;s somewhat expected to get a tip, especially when clients are considered &quot;difficult&quot; (though I&#x27;m unable to exactly quantify that).')
