Item(by='bikenaga', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612366640, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26013041, text='Mac Lane was one of the founders of category theory - see for example:<p>&quot;General Theory of Natural Equivalences&quot; - Samuel Eilenberg and Saunders MacLane. <i>Transactions of the American Mathematical Society</i>, Vol. 58, No. 2, (Sep., 1945), pp. 231-294<p>His &quot;Categorical algebra&quot; paper from 1964 is open-access:<p><a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;projecteuclid.org&#x2F;euclid.bams&#x2F;1183526392" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;projecteuclid.org&#x2F;euclid.bams&#x2F;1183526392</a><p>When his book came out it was the standard work for a while. I think some of the terminology he uses is no longer standard, and there are now many very good modern introductions. But his book is still a pretty good read, primarily because he was a wonderful writer with a very distinctive style.<p>That said, the examples (given the time the book was written) are almost exclusively mathematical. The proofs and exercises are usually not difficult, but at a high level of abstraction (not surprisingly).')
