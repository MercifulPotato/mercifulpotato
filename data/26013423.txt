Item(by='bryanlarsen', descendants=None, kids=[26018319], score=None, time=1612359133, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26010536, text='From a hardware POV, PC was more similar to S100 than it was to Apple II.   The S100 bus was a dumb extension of the 8080 pins, the PC a dumb extension of the 8088 pins and the Apple II a dumb extension of the 6502 pins.   The 6502 and 808x micros had very different timing cycles.<p>In comparison the fact that PC &amp; Apple II used a motherboard architecture while S100 used a backplane architecture is very superficial.  It was possible to buy S100 systems that used a motherboard architecture, it&#x27;s essentially just hardwiring a connector.  Here&#x27;s one:  <a href="http:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.s100computers.com&#x2F;Hardware%20Folder&#x2F;HeathZenith&#x2F;Motherboard&#x2F;Motherboard.htm" rel="nofollow">http:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.s100computers.com&#x2F;Hardware%20Folder&#x2F;HeathZenith&#x2F;M...</a><p>The other comparison point was in ecosystem: the S100 had a very rich ecosystem of vendors putting out systems and expansion cards, just like the PC Clone ecosystem.<p>Finally, the S100 and PC were open systems, whereas Apple was quite closed.<p>In 4 different ways I see PC as S100 v2.')
