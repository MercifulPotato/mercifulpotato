Item(by='eitland', descendants=None, kids=[25999414, 26000829], score=None, time=1612261944, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25999218, text='&gt; Because software engineers working for companies will know the struggle of trying to justify reducing technical debt, improving security practices, etc. but fail to see why these things are useful to them personally when the tables turn.<p>Problem is, ever so often what should reduce technical debt ends up also reducing &quot;technical assets&quot; even more.<p>Firefox is a perfect example of this, they reduced technical debt by selling of so many features that it was sometimes hard to see a single unique selling point except not being owned by what I think is the worlds largest data miner (and quitter)!<p>For someone who used to use and advocate Firefox this hurts.<p>What is left is certainly better than Chrome on a number of points (except being bug compatible with Chrome) but the differences are now so small on every point except &quot;not being owned by Google&quot; that they are hardly visible to non tech folks.<p>Edits: a bunch above, mostly to soften and clarify. Also:<p>IMO the current management at Mozilla inherited a fortune of goodwill, a best in class web browser and significant tech debt.<p>They supposedly traded goodwill and features for reduction in tech debt but a few years later I suspect they squandered most of it on coolness because if there was less tech debt now, some of the old features should now be trivial to add back.')
