Item(by='zamadatix', descendants=None, kids=[26003752], score=None, time=1612285728, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26003095, text='I did it on a fresh secondary install first just to get an understanding of what I wanted to do.  If you were going for a more prepackaged system like KDE it&#x27;s just a click but dwm -&gt; sway is basically rebuilding everything you did in dwm and (at least for me) couldn&#x27;t be done in one shot on a Saturday afternoon. I hear if you&#x27;re coming from i3 it&#x27;s dead simple but I was coming from KDE basically so had to figure everything out plus figure the wayland bits out.<p>As far as documentation I think this comment chain has the perfect set of links for that <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.reddit.com&#x2F;r&#x2F;swaywm&#x2F;comments&#x2F;l54a1a&#x2F;guidestutorials_for_sway_where_are_they&#x2F;gksvfui&#x2F;?utm_source=reddit&amp;utm_medium=web2x&amp;context=3" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;www.reddit.com&#x2F;r&#x2F;swaywm&#x2F;comments&#x2F;l54a1a&#x2F;guidestutori...</a> and the subreddit in general is a great source of information&#x2F;tips on it. In general it&#x27;s going to come down to 1) packages for sway, and wayland tools for things like clipboard&#x2F;screenshot&#x2F;xwayland&#x2F;etc, and wayland compatibility on different toolkits&#x2F;libraries 2) your sway config file which ties all of those packages together and personalizes the look, feel, and functionality of your workspace 3) envvars and other configurations to signal to GTK&#x2F;Firefox&#x2F;QT&#x2F;etc you want to prefer wayland native (otherwise a lot of things still default to X11 which will run via xwayland).<p>Right now I&#x27;d classify it as &quot;no harder than getting dwm&#x2F;i3 working the way you want it for the first time but a lot of work&quot;. Also like I said it gives the apps the ability to understand per monitor DPI or fractional DPI and so on, not it makes every app work perfectly. E.g. FF has really solid and performant GPU accelerated rendering and video decoding you can enable on top of webrender in Wayland but it&#x27;s a GTK app so if you have it run at 1.5x scale GTK is going to render it at 2x your screen resolution at 3x  your configured DPI and downscale the output by 2x to end up at your resolution with 1.5x scale like you asked. Not much different than when it was on X11, except it&#x27;s a bit more performant and they say it&#x27;s easier to maintain&#x2F;simpler than the X11 GPU acceleration code. On the flipside you have Chromium which now has the same GPU accelerated story in Wayland (with configuration) but understands how to do 1.5x without rescaling when running in Wayland and can move to another monitor that&#x27;s 1x and scale appropriately.<p>tl;dr: it&#x27;s a lot of work and a bit scary, don&#x27;t jump unless you&#x27;ve really got some time and interest. If you do jump it does do what it says on the tin though and apps are only going to get more native Wayland support with time not less.')
