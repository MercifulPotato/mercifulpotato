Item(by='tenebrisalietum', descendants=None, kids=[26009691, 26009466, 26008000], score=None, time=1612299162, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26005682, text='Well ...<p>The &quot;motherboard&quot; of most S100 systems AFAIK were simply a a power supply and bunch of slots.  Nothing onboard, your RAM fit in a slot, your CPU card fit in a slot, your video card or serial port for your terminal fit in a slot, your disk controller fit in a slot, etc.<p>The original IBM 5150 had RAM and CPU onboard, as well as a tape I&#x2F;O port.<p>I did run into an old Zenith PC clone at a Goodwill a while back.  I think dated from 1987.<p>Really weird system - the motherboard was an S100-style backplane that only had the Motorola MCxxx-whatever clock chip (a real one and even socketed).  On a big ISA-style card was a 286 with a lot of other chips, another big ISA-style card that looked like RAM, and a third that had all the discrete components of your PC chipset - the PIT, the two UART chips, the two DMA controllers.  I forget if the PICs were there or on the CPU board.<p>The BIOS was also weird ... had a built in monitor complete with assembler and disassembler commands.<p>Every single chip was socketed.  It was ... bizarre.  Had an MFM drive running DOS 2-point-something and an ISA VGA card in one of the slots.  Still worked.')
