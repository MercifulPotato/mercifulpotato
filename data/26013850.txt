The alternative that we are seeing here, of private surveillance is so much worse.<p>From time to time there&#x27;s a discussion on HN about the &quot;Nothing to Hide Argument&quot; (e.g [1],[2],[3]). On this topic, I feel there&#x27;s close to a unanimous position on HN that the &quot;nothing to hide argument&quot; is wrong. People mention counterexamples like &quot;are you ok with cameras in your bathroom&quot;, &quot;can you share your bank account password with me&quot;. They are all strawmen.<p>The way I see it is: if law enforcement identifies all the perpetrators of the Capitol attack and brings them to justice, that&#x27;s a good thing. I was not there. I don&#x27;t live in fear that one day the law enforcement will use some facebook photo of mine to send me to prison, because I don&#x27;t engage in criminal behavior. But if someone with my likeness was there, and a mob of redditors mistake that person to me, yes, that&#x27;s something that I can be afraid of.<p>[1] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=25416123" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=25416123</a><p>[2] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=4957864" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=4957864</a><p>[3] <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=25417117" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;news.ycombinator.com&#x2F;item?id=25417117</a>')
