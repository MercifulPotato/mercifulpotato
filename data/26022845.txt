Encumbering every piece of printed text with the copyright of the typefaces in question would be... problematic.<p>That said: programs which render text (this includes typical font formats like ttf which can have all kinds of kerning and hinting information so the fonts look good at multiple resolutions) <i>can</i> be copyrighted - they&#x27;re not <i>just</i> typefaces, after all - so you&#x27;re right that Adobe would take issue with just copying their font files.<p>&gt; Is the particular implementation of a font eligible for copyright but the &quot;design&quot; isn&#x27;t if I copy it and make my own font that looks the same?<p>That&#x27;s roughly my understanding, yes, although I&#x27;m not a lawyer.  It&#x27;s also worth noting that <i>trademark</i> can incorporate typefaces&#x2F;fonts - so if you&#x27;re making yet another sugary beverage, you might not want to pick the same distinctive typeface as Coca-Cola for your main logo.')
