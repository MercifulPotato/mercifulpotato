Item(by='jfk13', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612445753, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26024374, text='I would have assumed &quot;putter&quot; (in this sense) is onomatopoeic, from the sound of a small engine running slowly.<p>&quot;Potter&quot;, OTOH, comes from a word for poking or prodding at the ground (hence appropriate for gardening).<p>(I suppose with the right kind of ride-on mower, it&#x27;s possible to &quot;putter about the garden&quot; with appropriate sound effects!)<p>Checking OED citations, though, it seems the &quot;putter&quot; form (as a variant of &quot;potter&quot;, distinct from the sound of an engine &quot;puttering&quot;) does have a similar history (both go back to the 1820s).<p>Still, I&#x27;d agree that &quot;potter about the garden&quot; is by far the more common usage.')
