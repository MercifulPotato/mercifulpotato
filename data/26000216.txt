Item(by='roblabla', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612269101, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25999900, text='I wasn&#x27;t making any claim on whether this mitigation is actually useful, I was simply stating it&#x27;s unlikely to introduce more vulnerabilities than the status quo.<p>But since you&#x27;re asking, I can think of two upsides:<p>1. It restricts the kernel attack surface available to only those syscalls that are exposed through the wrapper. (This is obviously dubious if the libc provides a generic syscall function, I don&#x27;t know if OpenBSD libc has one).<p>2. It forces the ROP to either find the libc ASLR base, or to find a gadget that calls into the target libc function. This makes ROPs a lot harder to write.<p>As with any mitigations, they&#x27;re mostly meant to make the attacker&#x27;s life miserable. They&#x27;re not full protections, and can often be bypassed. The point is to increase the cost of the attack.')
