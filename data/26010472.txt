I&#x27;ve used Toptal a lot, and moonlight.com seems promising. I had a really wonderful client through the HN monthly freelancer thread. My current geographical area does not have much in the way of software, so I probably need to move to get local clients.<p>I would normally worry about finances, but I happened to be forced into it due to some (non-tech) classes I wanted to take, which was helpful because I&#x27;d already decided I was going to be burning money that year. And the previous year I was working on an iOS app on my own in Asia (= low cost of living), so I&#x27;d already discovered--a little to my surprise--that the sky didn&#x27;t fall in if you don&#x27;t have an income. I&#x27;ve discovered it usually takes me about a month to find a new client, but I&#x27;m sure that depends on the developer, experience, and subfield. If I wanted to do webdev it&#x27;d probably be a lot faster. If you want a conservative plan, I&#x27;d recommend saving 6 - 12 months of expenses, line up a client with the understanding that you would not be able to start for 2-4 weeks, and then quit. Or maybe you could arrange with your company to transition out more gradually. At some point, though, the water isn&#x27;t going to get any warmer, you just have to jump in the pool and scream underwater for a few seconds until you get used to it :)')
