When you get into commercial systems, they are faster, but there&#x27;s going to be a wait time that probably exceeds the time the customer is willing to sit around.  At the end of the day, the material&#x2F;physics[0] have more to do with &quot;how fast it can print&quot; than anything.<p>It&#x27;d make sense for them to offer some way to do this online and pick up in-store (or, hey, ship directly).  But, it might bring people into the stores or you may have people that want to &quot;feel&quot; examples of the material they&#x27;re printing the item in (assuming it&#x27;s offered with different options, which would be a good idea).<p>[0] I&#x27;m thinking FDM here, mostly, but you&#x27;re basically taking a material, heating it to the &quot;right&quot; melting point, applying it to a surface where gravity and controlled cooling cause the layer to adhere securely to the previous layer.  The printer can only print as fast as it can reliably melt&#x2F;adhere the material, any faster and you either under-extrude or fail to adhere and end up with spaghetti.')
