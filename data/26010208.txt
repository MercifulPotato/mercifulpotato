Item(by='dragontamer', descendants=None, kids=[26012250], score=None, time=1612322361, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26007795, text='&gt;  The first dose prevents hospitalization. Right now, for every second dose we give, that&#x27;s a decision to deny someone a life-saving dose in favor of someone already protected. What a strange prioritization to make.<p>Does 1 dose prevent the spread of COVID19?<p>If it requires 2-doses to get a true &quot;efficacy&quot; (lower spread), then it might be more worthwhile to push 2-doses, rather than 1-dose (where everyone gets &quot;less sick&quot;, but the virus continues to spread exponentially).<p>Real problem: 1-dose wasn&#x27;t really tested on a large &quot;phase 3&quot; style 10,000+ person test. Its impossible to answer this question (yet). It will take further testing to know.<p>----------<p>I think there&#x27;s something to be respected about sticking with what was tested, even if the scientific theory argues for a different approach.<p>Just because the theory says so, doesn&#x27;t mean that the theory is true (at least, not until the theory is well tested).<p>Doing something different and hoping for the best just because its kinda correct theoretically is... overly optimistic. I wouldn&#x27;t necessarily be against it, but it makes sense to stick with what was formally tested.')
