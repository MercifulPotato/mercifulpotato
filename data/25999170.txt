Item(by='simiones', descendants=None, kids=[25999480, 25999647, 25999192], score=None, time=1612260659, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25998807, text='The multiverse usually refers to multiple universes each with their own big bang, existing in regular space, but outside our light cone. It is a completely unscientific theory, in the sense that there is no chance of ever proving or denying it, by its very construction.<p>The many worlds interpretation is completely unrelated, and it doesn&#x27;t suffer from this problem of being unfalsifiable even in principle. However, I would also not say that the MWI is a straightforward deduction from the laws of QM. It rejects the most visible effect of those laws (the Born rule, i.e. that they predict probabilities of classical events and not certainties) and replaces it with an infinity of &quot;worlds&quot;. Even worse, it still doesn&#x27;t explain WHY we can only &quot;see&quot; one such &quot;world&quot;, while elementary particles can &quot;see&quot; all of them (i.e. it doesn&#x27;t solve the measurement problem any more than Copenhagen interpretation). And there is also debate on how plausible it is to even define the &quot;probability of a world&quot;, so whether MWI is actually consistent with the Born rule is somewhat in doubt.')
