Item(by='snidane', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612252235, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25996645, text='With shared-disk the data sits in cold storage (eg. s3) for which you pay cheaply. Only when you have to do some big computation you spin up a temporary compute cluster, do the computation, store resukt back in s3 and kill the cluster. You can also have long lived compute clusters and shut them or scale them down at night when nobody uses them. Since the data doesn&#x27;t &quot;live&quot; in the compute nodes, you can add or remove as many compute nodes as you wish.<p>In shared-nothing the data &quot;lives&quot; on the compute nodes, so you can&#x27;t willy nilly add or remove nodes. The data would either get lost if you removed more nodes than what is necessary for triple replicated redundancy. Or if you added nodes you&#x27;d have to wait before the data gets rebalanced to those new nodes, resulting in massive reshuffling of everything.<p>Keep in mind that shared-nothing clusters are most likely long lived and have multiple large datasets sitting on them, so by adding nodes you will start to shuffle everything around.<p>With shared-disk your compute cluster is only a single use for one dataset and you don&#x27;t lose data as you scale cluster up and down.<p>There is a hybrid architecture which will give you both, but doesn&#x27;t exist out there: Shared-disk with active caching. Ie. giving you control over which tables will be pinned down on your temporary compute-cluster. That will give you performance of shared-nothing but with convenience of shared-disk temporary compute clusters.')
