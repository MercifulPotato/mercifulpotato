Item(by='ajross', descendants=None, kids=[26004105], score=None, time=1612287800, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26003887, text='So, a short squeeze is a real thing.  The stock going up means that the people holding an underwater short need to rush to buy to close their positions before they go farther underwater.  This then makes the stock go up faster.  That&#x27;s indeed what happened last week.<p>But you don&#x27;t need to buy back all the stock, just enough such that you have enough flexibility to hold the stock across the inevitable peak and drop.  In a consumer brokerage, this generally takes the form of a &quot;margin call&quot; and the brokerage will often buy stock for you out of your margin so they don&#x27;t get caught holding the bag, and eventually can seize your whole portfolio to make themselves whole (the regulations there get complicated and I&#x27;m not expert).  It&#x27;s an absolute thing, and you lose all your money.  But that&#x27;s not how it works for a hedge fund, the nature of which is to have access to financing regular people don&#x27;t.  They can just cut a deal with bigger players to get through, and that&#x27;s how Melvin seems to have managed this.<p>To be clear: Melvin made an outrageously inappropriate bet, got caught, and lots a ton of money.  But they&#x27;re out now and the story is over.  Now GME is just a bubble like any other bubble, fed by naive investors believing it will go up when it won&#x27;t.<p>As far as &quot;what happens if WSB doesn&#x27;t sell?&quot;, the answer is nothing.  WSB doesn&#x27;t hold the full capitalization of GME.  There are plenty of other shares out there being traded, and the price of those trades is what you see on the ticker.')
