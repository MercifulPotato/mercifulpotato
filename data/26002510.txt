Item(by='tialaramex', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612281505, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000258, text='For symmetric encryption, as your parent explained, yes, simply double the key length and you&#x27;re fine. (In practice you might get away with less than double because it seems unlikely such quantum computers, even if eventually constructed, would be similarly cheap to conventional computers)<p>But for asymmetric (public key) encryption the larger key lengths barely help, so you&#x27;d need infeasibly enormous keys.  e.g. terabyte RSA keys have been &quot;proposed&quot; facetiously for this purpose.<p>Thus, Post Quantum Encryption research is mostly interested in finding solutions for two asymmetric problems: Key Agreement (a replacement for today&#x27;s Elliptic Curve Diffie Hellman) and Signatures (a replacement for things like ECDSA) that would survive an attack by a larger quantum computer able to execute Shor&#x27;s algorithm.')
