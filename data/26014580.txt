Item(by='quotemstr', descendants=None, kids=[26022865], score=None, time=1612366757, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26009733, text='&gt; My binaries work on six different operating systems. In order to do that I had to roll my own C library.<p>Other people have made software for decades without writing program-specific libc instances. Tell me you at least started with something decent like musl instead of literally writing your own libc from printf on up.<p>&gt;  Linux should not be putting anything into the address space that the executable does not specify<p>Execution has to start somewhere, and kernels have often reserved parts of the system address space for themselves.<p>&gt; The main thing dynamic shared objects accomplish is giving upstream dependencies leverage over your software.<p>Loose binding in interfaces allows systems on both sides of the interface to evolve. If you want 100% complete control over your system for some reason instead of writing programs that play well with others, just ship your thing as a VM image and be done with it.')
