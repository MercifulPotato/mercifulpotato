I know there&#x27;s definitely a scale of quality&#x2F;practicality, ranging from stuff like burgers to beer chicken to boned cuts (t-bones, ribs) to brisket&#x2F;pulled pork&#x2F;friends, but I thought it was all part of the same umbrella.<p>As a comparison, in Brazil, the homely variety of barbecue will usually not feature things like cupim (beef hump), which it&#x27;s a staple of barbecue restaurants. They&#x27;re all considered barbecue regardless, though.<p>To go back to the idea of authenticity and grammar, I don&#x27;t believe chicken hearts are part of american barbecue fare anywhere in the US, picnic-style or not, whereas in Brazil they are (though typically only in restaurants). Similarly, you&#x27;d pretty much never see hotdogs being associated w&#x2F; barbecue in Brazil, and they&#x27;d have vinaigrette and potato chip strips <i>in</i> them.')
