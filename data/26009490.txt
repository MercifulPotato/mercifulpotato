Item(by='sliken', descendants=None, kids=[26017166], score=None, time=1612315351, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26005616, text='I&#x27;ve worked with a researchers who use LIDAR heavily.  Amazing technology, but aside from cost&#x2F;weight&#x2F;size&#x2F;expense there&#x27;s one big disadvantage.  LIDAR doesn&#x27;t work like eyes.<p>So in certain conditions LIDAR can see better than human eyes, like say complete darkness.  This could lead to a car behaving differently than a human driver which might well cause an accident.<p>In other conditions LIDAR can see much worse than a human.  Depending on the fog, snow, rain, dust, tumbleweeds etc can look more solid to a lidar than a human eye.  Which can also cause accidents.  A human might recognize a whisp of fog, whose outline and volume look like a fallen tree, but a camera would have a better chance.<p>So even with a magic LIDAR that costs $100, has good range, solid state, etc. it&#x27;s not clear what LIDAR offers over a pair of cameras, or other cheap sensors like cameras, mm band (60 GHz) radar, and ultrasound.<p>Additionally LIDAR systems need cameras anyways.  Is that a ambulance or  UPS truck?  Schoolbus or city bus?  Police car or Camry?  Does the police car have it&#x27;s lights on? \nDid someone flash their high beams at you?  Delivery truck or post office truck?  Color is hugely important in predicting what&#x27;s going to happen.  Sure you can add cameras to LIDAR, but then why?  Doubly is you have two cameras for distance.<p>So I&#x27;d love to be wrong, and indisputably LIDAR is getting better, but seems like pairs of cameras might well be the best fit for driving like a human.  Doesn&#x27;t seem like it could ever be LIDAR, but LIDAR + cameras could compete, but what does LIDAR give you over a pair of cameras?')
