Item(by='simiones', descendants=None, kids=[26016024], score=None, time=1612370729, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26015122, text='&gt; Rationalizations about &quot;getting the job done&quot; or &quot;ease of use&quot; are the same as saying macOS has a better UI than a given GNU&#x2F;Linux distro, and that&#x27;s why it&#x27;s OK Apple sends third party app usage data without you knowing.<p>This is patently false. It&#x27;s not a &quot;rationalization&quot; to say &quot;there is no &#x27;free&#x27; tool that gets the job done&quot;. It is in fact the most important criterion: we need to be able to teach students, in a way that doesn&#x27;t hurt any more than the situation already is. We need to help schools deal with extremely limited IT budgets without hurting any more than they already are.<p>If there are free and proprietary tools that get the job done just as well (including ease of use, cost of maintenance, accessibility and others), then it&#x27;s ok to ask schools to standardize on the free tools. If the free tools are worse at the actual job than the proprietary tools, then it would UNETHICAL for schools to insist on the inferior choice. Of course, if it is the other way around, if the proprietary software is inferior for the purpose, then it is equally unethical to push for that!<p>However, none of this makes it ok for the proprietary tools to hurt their users&#x27; privacy, like the Apple tools were. In fact, it makes it even worse.')
