Item(by='dragontamer', descendants=None, kids=[26020240], score=None, time=1612373175, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26015314, text='&gt; &quot;Pie tastes better than cake!&quot;<p>Indeed. And is Cheesecake a pie or a cake? You bake cheesecake in pie-crust, but it has cake in the name.<p>The memory-management &quot;its both&quot; scheme is reference-counting. In some contexts, its ref-counts are considered manual (C++ shared_ptr, Rust). In other contexts, its considered automated garbage collection (Python, Lisp).<p>In all cases: tons of atomic add&#x2F;subtract + memory-barriers to strictly order reference counts between many threads... making it actually kind of bad for multithreaded code compared to other garbage collection schemes. (Each shared_ptr&lt;&gt; = blah() requires an atomic add and atomic-subtract. So you actually have escalating costs the more you use the shared_ptr). I think the circular-issue is kind of overblown: it certainly happens (doubly-linked lists, graphs, trees which store a &quot;root pointer&quot; or even an &quot;iterator&quot; somewhere inside of them) but people know how to use weak_ptr&lt;&gt; these days and avoid most issues. (Well... except for arbitrary graphs. No way to know where to put weak_ptr&lt;&gt; there. But most &quot;graph heavy&quot; algorithms use other representations: dense matrix or sparse matrix forms... and probably should avoid slow &#x2F; low-performance general purpose memory allocators anyway)<p>-----------<p>Once you recognize the multithreaded-sync issues associated with shared_ptr&lt;&gt; &#x2F; RefCount, you start building a thread that &quot;centralizes&quot; the ref-counts in a reader&#x2F;writer queue to ensure that objects are deleted at the right time. Oh wait, that&#x27;s called a garbage collector thread and you&#x27;ve suddenly moved to mark&amp;sweep accidentally.<p>Once you get a garbage collector thread, its not too big of a leap to start thinking about multiple garbage-collector threads (scaling to higher garbage collection performance). I mean, yeah, I&#x27;m glossing over all sorts of high-performance multithreaded lock-free datastructures here, but lets pretend those implementation details are solved. Lol.<p>-----------<p>As usual: its not really the garbage collection or memory-management parts that are hard. Its the multithreaded high-performance (and provably correct!) portion that&#x27;s really, really hard. Even for simple ideas like ref-counts or even &quot;manual&quot; malloc&#x2F;free.')
