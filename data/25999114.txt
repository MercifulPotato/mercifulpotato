Item(by='XorNot', descendants=None, kids=[26002864], score=None, time=1612260270, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25998584, text='The US in fact did have a serious problem with nuclear warplan targeting during the Cold War - there was essentially never a reason you could give to take a target <i>off</i> the list, so the plan for a good deal of time was essentially something like &quot;hit every single conceivable target several times&quot; (to account for failed bombs or losses en route).<p>It was actually one of Dick Cheney&#x27;s initiatives to create a process which would <i>remove</i> targets from the nuclear warplan&#x27;s to get the number of weapons used down to a manageable number - and end the feedback of &quot;oh no we&#x27;ve assigned all the weapons - we need more weapons in case there&#x27;s more targets&quot;.<p>EDIT: A critical element with nuclear warplan&#x27;s is that you don&#x27;t have one bomb per target - you generally have several bombs per target via different delivery systems with different priorities, because the key element of MAD is survivability - there has to always be a guarantee in your adversary&#x27;s mind that they cannot destroy enough capability to create a winning scenario for them.')
