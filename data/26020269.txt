Item(by='wegs', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612392559, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26017684, text='Owners.<p>What a concept.<p>The owners are the shareholders.<p>GameStop was started as a side gig by Riggio, who was also the founder of Barnes and Noble. He has bigger things to worry about, like his retirement. It&#x27;s been run by professional executives for the most part.<p>DeMatteo is still around, but I think that&#x27;s it for the founding team. If you can call a series of merges a founding.<p>Yeah, if I own a company, I&#x27;ll sweat and bleed to make it successful. That&#x27;s the #1 founder &#x2F; startup advantage.<p>That&#x27;s not true for publicly-traded companies. Those are an abstraction. If a CEO is making $35 mil and someone offers them $50 mil, guess where they&#x27;re going?<p>A lot of startups don&#x27;t realize that as a source of advantage, but it&#x27;s a big one. It&#x27;s a lot of where the agility comes from too. A professional CEO need to first worry about keeping their job, then about their next job, and finally, the organization. For a founder, for the most part, those are one and the same.<p>If you gave me a failing public business as an owner -- with no accountability, no board to please, no existing executive team to back stab me, and all the upside -- I&#x27;d do better than most CEOs in turning it around. That&#x27;s not because I&#x27;m more qualified or competent, but because I&#x27;d have the freedom of being an owner. The original CEO, if properly incentived, would usually do a better job than I would, in turn.')
