The kinds of white supremacists under discussion here are thought to want to exert force, especially with weapons. We don&#x27;t arm social services.<p>That said, there is a dangerous undercurrent of white supremacy in social services. It&#x27;s of the nominally benevolent kind, where social workers bring biases and don&#x27;t understand the communities they&#x27;re serving. One example, it may seem that a white family is helping a black child by adopting it away from a much poorer black family with issues, but there are a lot of things a white family doesn&#x27;t know how to prepare black children for, from their hair to dealing with police.<p>This is an incredibly difficult and thorny subject. I bring up one example that I happen to have a very distant and passing familiarity with, but don&#x27;t intend it to give more than a vague hint of the complexity.<p>I bring it up only because, while it&#x27;s not likely to be infiltrated by hate groups, white supremacy is a systemic problem in the US, including social work. Dealing with that requires constant feedback and self-inquiry, and ensuring that minority voices are genuinely listened to in the process.')
