Item(by='Hemospectrum', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612389271, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26018194, text='Gundam was always about societies experiencing large-scale changes that no individual person has a handle on, and the struggles of people who try to make a difference anyway. In that sense, it&#x27;s comparable to The Expanse, and the &quot;magical&quot; elements serve more or less the same purpose.<p>Planetes, on the other hand, isn&#x27;t much like either of these. Its subject matter is intensely personal, and even when the characters occasionally get involved in high-stakes situations, it&#x27;s less to do with changes in society (which occur much more slowly) and more to do with someone turning their small-scale private difficulties into everybody else&#x27;s problem. The political elements intrude for a while and disappear again, and I don&#x27;t remember them nearly as well as the bits about a lost watch, or a marriage proposal.<p>In a nutshell, if Gundam and The Expanse need magic because they&#x27;re larger-than-life, Planetes has no use for it, because it&#x27;s exactly-the-same-size-as-life.')
