Item(by='mattnewton', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612316653, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26009042, text='I am not an expert but this seems different from the short selling we&#x27;re talking about. In short selling of a stock, the stock is borrowed and sold immediately for profit. There is no productive action the short seller engages in to recover the stock, they just rebuy it on the open market, or not at all if the company has gone bankrupt.<p>If you are really stretching it, they can produce research that shows the stock should be worth less in this time, without committing any crime- which means it is based entirely on public information the market already has. Bashing a company could be considered a productive activity we are incentivizing from a certain point of view. But their incentives are all towards tearing down the underlying security.<p>In the agricultural example, the person selling the futures to their goods has to actually produce the goods. While someone might be borrowing the stock to resell it, ultimately the end farmer has to produce new value to deliver on their future contract. Otherwise, they would just sell futures to their crops, and then take their money to do nothing! But this is exactly what someone who short sells a stock does. They take the money, and they just sit around waiting for the stock to drop, no doubt resisting the urge to make it drop themselves through legally gray methods to increase their profits.')
