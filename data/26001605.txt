Item(by='GlenTheMachine', descendants=None, kids=[26002422, 26002445, 26002260, 26003532], score=None, time=1612277659, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000380, text='There&#x27;s a lot of conspiracy theory-type stuff on this thread, but the truth is a lot more mundane. Essentially, this is a combination of an org that doesn&#x27;t do a lot of basic research combined with bureaucracy at work.<p>I&#x27;m a DoD scientist. The funding levels listed here are at the low end for an in-house basic research project. The funding levels would support 1-2 scientists half time, including their overhead rates. Once the funding is granted it normally runs for three years without a significant review.<p>They funded it originally because the boards that decide whether to fund these programs are staffed entirely in house by folks who aren&#x27;t really researchers, and who have an incentive to keep their own people employed. They were &quot;silent&quot; about it because to release any information whatsoever about a program, no matter how mundane, requires at minimum a multi-week review process. You can&#x27;t just answer a reporter&#x27;s questions over the phone. And any such review normally requires the researcher to initiate it.<p>So: they had a researcher who was out there, but probably had done interesting work in the past and was a good salesman. He convinced a board of non-scientists to fund him at what the Navy considers a very low level for three years. He didn&#x27;t produce any publishable work so he didn&#x27;t have an incentive to talk about it, and the Navy center involved didn&#x27;t push him to because they have no incentive to do so.')
