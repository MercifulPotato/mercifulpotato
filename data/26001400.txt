Item(by='dragontamer', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612276669, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25996388, text='Hmmm...<p>The paper &quot;A Fast New DES Implementation in Software&quot; calculated 64-parallel DES Round across a 64-bit register in 1,040 DEC Alpha instructions. There are 16 rounds per DES, and then another 2,500 clock ticks to convert the bitwise representation back into normal form.<p>The key to understanding things:<p>&gt; The full circuit of DES contains about 16000 gates (including the key scheduling, which costs nothing), and thus we can compute DES 64 times in about\n16000 instructions on 64-bit processors.<p>Each logic-gate can be seen as an AND&#x2F;OR&#x2F;XOR instruction on a 1-bit computer. Use 64-bit wide AND&#x2F;OR&#x2F;XOR to do 64x calculations in parallel. The modern equivalent would be 256x in parallel (AVX2 for Threadripper), AVX512 for Skylake-X, and 1024x in parallel for NVidia Ampere.<p>I dunno what kind of post-processing would need to be done, so I&#x27;ll estimate an even 20,000 clock ticks per 64x DES result on the old DEC Alpha.<p>A modern GPU can probably execute 1024-bit per clock tick (AMD RDNA &#x2F; NVidia Ampere 32-way x 32-bit), while a modern AVX512 CPU can execute 512-bit at a time. And AVX2 is 256-bit at a time (but probably faster: AVX2 &#x2F; AVX512 execute XOR &#x2F; ADDs at a rate of MORE than one clock tick per clock cycle).<p>The paper was very innovative: the SBox was a 100-instruction long sequence of AND &#x2F; OR &#x2F; XOR instructions. Avoiding the cache probably is a good thing, but I do wonder if there&#x27;s a way to benefit from memory somehow.<p>* ~80 AVX2 clock ticks per result (20k clock ticks &#x2F; 256-bits)<p>* ~40 AVX512 clock ticks per result (20k clocks &#x2F; 512 bits)<p>* ~20 GPU clock ticks per result (20k clocks &#x2F; 1024 bits)<p>So a 64-core AMD at 4GHz probably could do ~3.2-billion DES attempts per second.<p>A 216 SM NVidia A100 at 1.4 GHz probably can do ~15-billion DES attempts per second.<p>---------------<p>Hmmmmmm.... the results this page claims is ~16-billion DES attempts per second. Which does seem to be approximately what I&#x27;d expect a concerted effort on an NVidia A100 would get. I&#x27;m doing very &quot;napkin-level&quot; math here with tons of assumptions.<p>The SBox-lookup (100-AND&#x2F;OR&#x2F;XOR gates) might be better run on AVX &#x2F; AVX512 however, because AMD Threadripper can do 4x simple AND&#x2F;OR&#x2F;XOR instructions per clock tick, and IIRC Intel AVX512 can do 3x simple bitwise instructions per clock tick. With the proper &quot;cut dependencies&quot;, an SBox probably can be really executed in under 40 clock ticks instead of 100. CPUs could very well cut out hundreds of cycles from the above estimate.<p>The S-Box is very curious for DES: it is a non-reversible 6-bit lookup with a 4-bit result.')
