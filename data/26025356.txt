Item(by='Twisol', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612447323, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26025350, text='&quot;Algebraic data types&quot; refers to having both product and sum types, where products are the records&#x2F;structs that just about every language has, and sums are indeed the &quot;discriminated unions&quot; under discussion.<p>Most languages don&#x27;t have built-in syntax for sum types, so most developers are familiar with sum types only by the shadows cast by a variety of encodings. One of them is the subject of the OP, the Visitor pattern. Another is the &quot;tagged union&quot; seen in C, effectively:<p><pre><code>    struct Rectangle { int x, y, w, h; }\n    struct Circle { int x, y, r; }\n\n    struct Shape {\n      enum {\n        RectangleTag,\n        CircleTag,\n      } tag;\n\n      union {\n        struct Rectangle rectangle;\n        struct Circle circle;\n      };\n    };\n</code></pre>\nWhich doesn&#x27;t look too bad until you realize I haven&#x27;t explained how to <i>use</i> the thing, and then it&#x27;s similarly involved and even more delicate than the Visitor pattern.')
