Item(by='themodelplumber', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612313338, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26009122, text='I always like to see these. A good code of conduct benefits all kinds of people and generally brings awareness to the general level of quality of a project. It also helps to onboard people who genuinely want to participate in a community but who may suffer from a lack of experience.<p>Even for individuals who strive to live by a high-quality subjective code of ethics, and for whom a code of ethics is a big &quot;why is that even necessary (because I&#x27;m already a good person and you already should be, too)&quot;--it&#x27;s good to get familiar with external standards and learn to engage with them.<p>There are groups of people for whom a lack of attention to the external&#x2F;objective&#x2F;group-shared standard is simply a no-go, and yes, there are people for whom the lack of a shared code is an unfortunate invitation to engage in unappreciated behavior. The &quot;we&quot; is more important than the &quot;I&quot; in many ways, and sometimes the &quot;we&quot; has to put its foot down, and if that seems a bit ridiculous and broad, so be it, but at least it&#x27;s something.<p>This particular code seems to embrace an overarching dichotomy, or dimension, from &quot;expected behavior&quot; to &quot;unacceptable behavior.&quot; In my experience the Foundation will probably benefit from additional perspectives and even additional dichotomies which deepen and enhance the code over time. It&#x27;s good to be flexible, prospective, and forward-looking with these kinds of things. Anyway, I&#x27;m glad to see it.')
