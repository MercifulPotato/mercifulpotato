Item(by='TheOtherHobbes', descendants=None, kids=[26024662], score=None, time=1612432846, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26023795, text='No idea why this is being downvoted, because it&#x27;s correct. You can&#x27;t publish letters without permission. This has been tested in court many times.<p>What you can do is sell the physical object. That belongs to you.<p>You can also burn it or destroy it - which is an interesting edge case.<p>This really comes down to different kinds of ownership. The whole point of copyright is that creative&#x2F;cultural&#x2F;intellectual property rights are a thing, analogous to tangible property but with a different set of legal and commercial rights and obligations.<p>Electronic media depend on context. ISP and telcos are usually considered common carriers so they don&#x27;t own the copyright of emails or SMSs.<p>Social media networks either claim all copyright in full (bad news for you...) or have a specific license clause in the T&amp;Cs which gives them reproduction rights.<p>Sometimes these are for specific limited purposes, and sometimes they&#x27;re a blanket sign-away of all exploitation rights. (Bad news for you again.)')
