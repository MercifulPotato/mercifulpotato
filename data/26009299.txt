Item(by='tialaramex', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612313845, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26005805, text='I&#x27;m not really imagining any &quot;motivation&quot; beyond the intellectual exercise of proving America spends lots of money on things it actually doesn&#x27;t spend money on, by squinting until the whole world is blurry.<p>Economists would like everything to be dollars, because that means you can plug numbers into a spreadsheet and get answers that work. Centuries of this not working haven&#x27;t dissuaded them and I don&#x27;t expect my rant to change that.<p>People who actually think we should care about population welfare do <i>not</i> try to squint at the dollar figures like this paper, you&#x27;ll see them looking at policies qualitatively rather than quantitatively, and this way you don&#x27;t need to draw a dozen charts to come to the apparently startling revelation that the US is a wealthy country in which money is being stolen from the poor to be wasted by the rich and this is a bad idea.<p>Sometimes this sort of work will be excused on the rationale that it will guide political decision making. This is an error perhaps everywhere and certainly in the US. A Republican is not going to read this paper and say &quot;Aha! My policies were mistaken, I can actually have better impact by reducing tax breaks on the wealthy and directing the increased income to the poorest&quot;. They&#x27;re going to glance at it, conclude it does not support the policies they wish to enact and so it&#x27;s irrelevant.')
