Item(by='simias', descendants=None, kids=[26001403, 26001307, 26002430], score=None, time=1612273266, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26000394, text='I understand that but if they&#x27;re going to go down that route for OpenBSD why not bring it to all platforms? While I can believe that going the raw syscall route can be easier than dealing with libc idiosyncrasies, it seems to me that maintaining both interfaces depending on the target OS would be trickier than just cutting your losses and going the libc route everywhere.<p>After all as far I can tell it&#x27;s not just an OpenBSD problem since famously they got breakage in MacOS as well.<p>I must admit that I haven&#x27;t taken the time to analyze in depth the pros and cons here, but Go&#x27;s history of NIH coupled with the fact that basically every other mainstream language manages to work by binding the libc leaves me very perplex.<p>In particular some of the points raised in the blogpost I linked seem fishy to me. For instance errno being a global: this is in no way a Go-specific problem (multithreaded C couldn&#x27;t run concurrent syscalls if it was true). In practice errno is thread-local instead of being a true global. It&#x27;s explicitly documented in the man page:<p>&gt; errno  is  defined  by  the ISO C standard to be a modifiable lvalue of type int, and must not be explicitly declared; errno may  be  a  macro. errno  is  thread-local;  setting  it in one thread does not affect its value in any other thread.<p>I can believe that there are other issues I fail to consider, but again it works for everybody else, what makes Go so special here?')
