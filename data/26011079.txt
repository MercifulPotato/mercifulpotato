Item(by='wahern', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612332763, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26010771, text='I don&#x27;t think the Sputnik V vaccine has any substantive relationship to phage research and therapy. And I don&#x27;t think there&#x27;s much reason to doubt that Russian researchers <i>could</i> develop a solid vaccine, if only because it&#x27;s difficult to say with strong confidence which approaches would <i>definitely</i> fail. Rather, it&#x27;s a question of whether they <i>actually</i> <i>did</i> given the political dynamics. There&#x27;s good reason to discount the credibility of claims coming from authoritarian regimes. (Reasons for which most Americans have a renewed appreciation, rightly or wrongly.)<p>I suspect it wouldn&#x27;t be too difficult to fudge the numbers (e.g. candidate selection criteria) in such a way that a mediocre vaccine looks excellent. Until it&#x27;s reproduced or until other opportunities for falsification arise, trusting a result is largely a matter of the personal credibility of the researchers and, importantly, the system in which they operate.')
