Item(by='didibus', descendants=None, kids=[26010975, 26010942], score=None, time=1612329672, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=26005758, text='One thing that wasn&#x27;t covered here is that productivity is about:<p>&gt; the effectiveness of productive effort<p>and<p>&gt; the state or quality of producing something<p>Those are quoted from the dictionary definition of productivity, and that definition in my opinion outlines a great insight. Productivity is about the &quot;product&quot; first and foremost.<p>One thing that&#x27;s often missing is teams don&#x27;t have ways to quantify how good the product itself is. Most teams will instead pivot to trying to measure their rate of change to the product. This doesn&#x27;t mean you have become more productive, because software product is not like food production, more of it is not always better. Better software will instead be about it being more ingenious, more intuitive, more tailored to the problems of its users, more responsive, with fewer malfunctions, etc.<p>So to me, this whole thing of trying to measure &quot;productivity&quot; which disregards the product from the equation is incomplete. It&#x27;s trying to measure developer efficiency at changing things, without care if the changes are for better or worse. This includes things like measuring velocity, or line of code, or ticket closed. But it also includes what this article proposes, number of meetings, time to complete code reviews, developer satisfaction with their tools, etc.<p>All these are trying to see how quickly can developer make changes without ever measuring if the change produces a better product, thus if it actually made the team more productive at effectively positively improving the product.<p>I&#x27;d like to hear about ways to measure software product state and quality. If we had those, you&#x27;d have an easy way to know how productive a team would be by seeing how quickly they can improve the product.')
