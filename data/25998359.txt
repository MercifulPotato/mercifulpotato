Item(by='spijdar', descendants=None, kids=[25999381, 25998498, 25998557], score=None, time=1612252656, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25998258, text='Okay, so I&#x27;m referring mainly to the typical &quot;GNU&#x2F;Linux&quot; desktop&#x2F;server OS vs embedded or &quot;container Linux&quot; which is where the majority of alternative libc use will happen.<p>In either case though there is <i>a</i> libc that most software will use, and the mitigations can be applied there. Even though direct use of syscalls is legal on Linux, the fact that it&#x27;s stable is primarily relevant and interesting to said libc developers.<p>The fact that syscalls aren&#x27;t guaranteed on other systems is usually of little consequence since the libc is developed in tandem with the kernels of those systems. Linux&#x27;s situation as a fully decoupled kernel means it does things differently in that sense. The developers are fully separated, so there needs to be a strong &quot;contract&quot; that syscalls will be stable.<p>Doesn&#x27;t mean (IMO) it&#x27;s a good idea for end-users e.g. software developers to use syscalls except in exceptional circumstances. Which is usually the case!')
