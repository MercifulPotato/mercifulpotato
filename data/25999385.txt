Item(by='j1elo', descendants=None, kids=None, score=None, time=1612262466, title=None, item_type='comment', url=None, parent=25999218, text='Save the obvious distance, I kind of see a bit of similarity with the Python 2 to 3 transition; it breaks downstream programs and processes that shouldn&#x27;t break.<p>Another similarity I see is that Xorg is like the Linux Kernel of GUI applications. Ideally, you just shouldn&#x27;t break &quot;userspace&quot;.<p>I agree that some stuff is definitely broken in Xorg, like High DPI setups. But I also believe that&#x27;s no reason to break a myriad other setups that are working well, <i>right now</i>.<p>This constant &quot;1 step forward, 2 steps back&quot; (from the point of view of the final consumers, who really only care about the result, and not the means) is so common in the ecosystem of GNU&#x2F;Linux based O.S. It&#x27;s also why I see the mantra of the Linux kernel so utterly fascinating and inspiring. It takes real work to build something and keep it working for <i>decades</i> with a minimum of breakage; starting from scratch looks then like child&#x27;s play in comparison.<p>I guess the problem is the same as always: money. In a parallel universe where Xorg is at the base of Windows, Microsoft would just throw millions at it to improve the architecture and fix issues, while at the same time trying to limit breaking changes as much as possible... but that requires willingness to do it, and deep pockets.')
